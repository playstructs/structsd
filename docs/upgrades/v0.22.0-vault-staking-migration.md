## Held unbondings after maturity

Held unbondings require continued reconciliation after maturity. SDK `UnbondingCanComplete` can pay a matured entry when the last hold is
released without invoking a Structs staking hook. A one-time maturity sweep
therefore left permanent nonzero `Defusing` after a late release. Reconciliation
now reuses its existing unbonding read to schedule one retry after the current
block time whenever a matured entry remains held. Retries share the bounded
maturity queue and stop once release is observed.

`TestMigratedHeldUnbondingClearsDefusingAfterLateRelease` creates a native
undelegation with two holds, migrates its shares and pending record into the
vault, and runs actual maturity blocks. Repeated held checks retain one retry
row. Releasing one hold through the SDK pays nothing; releasing the last pays
the vault and removes the native unbonding ID. The following committed block
clears defusing while preserving remaining fuel, power and historical weight,
without another credit. The test models an external module releasing after the
Structs block phase through the SDK API; it does not submit a signed hold-release
transaction or exercise a specific external module. SDK accounting checks pass.

## Retained vesting self-stake: accounting constraints

The split-account investigation in this section is historical. The approved
implementation keeps the whole native account intact, preserving its shared
schedule and counters without implementing the coordinated-account design below.

The remaining refusal is not just an address-transfer limitation. The SDK stores
vesting counters per account, not per validator, and evaluates continuous vesting
with `LegacyDec` and `RoundInt`. Keeping self-stake at the operator while moving
other assets therefore divides a formerly shared vesting obligation.

Two executable counterexamples in
`app/upgrades/v0_22_0/vault_vesting_split_test.go` establish why a proportional
split into ordinary SDK accounts is insufficient:

- A two-coin continuous schedule has vested one coin at second 51 of 100. Two
  one-coin schedules have each vested one coin at the same instant. Original
  amounts sum correctly, but the split unlocks an extra coin early.
- Ten coins are fully delegated while vesting. At the halfway point, five remain
  unvested. Splitting the account into six-coin and four-coin schedules happens to
  round exactly, and their delegated-vesting counters still sum to ten. After
  refunding five coins from the six-coin side, the old single account has no
  locked coins, while the two-account arrangement locks two of the refunded
  coins. Even the post-refund delegated-vesting totals still match.

The second example is independent of the first: preserving the exact sum of
vesting curves does not preserve the liquidity of shared delegation counters.
SDK `LockedCoinsFromVesting` is `max(vesting - delegatedVesting, 0)` per account;
summing that expression after a split differs from applying it before the split.
`TrackUndelegation` also consumes delegated-free counters before delegated-vesting
counters, without knowing which original validator position supplied either.

Implementation must address schedule evaluation and counter ownership separately.
Do not rescale native continuous schedules and assume their rounded outputs add
up, duplicate a complete schedule on both accounts, or infer historical vesting
counters from current slash-reduced stake. A representation that evaluates the
original schedule and assigns exact complementary obligations avoids independent
rounding, but it does not by itself solve the refund/liquidity counterexample.
Shared accounting or an explicit treatment of obligations when retained stake is
refunded is still needed. Additional locking is a gameplay/economic consequence,
not evidence of preserving the old semantics; it must not be hidden behind a
passing conservation check.

These tests pass against the pinned SDK and constrain the implementation design;
they do not implement retained vesting self-stake. The migration still refuses
that state atomically. This is an unresolved engineering design, not a finding
that migration is impossible and not a new request for user authorization.

### Native refund boundary

The pinned SDK's `staking/keeper/delegation.go:CompleteUnbonding` calls bank
`UndelegateCoinsFromModuleToAccount`. Bank `UndelegateCoins` debits the module,
loads the destination account, calls its `TrackUndelegation`, persists that
account and credits its bank balance. It does not go through `SendCoins`, and
`CompleteUnbonding` has no subsequent staking hook for the completed refund.

`app/vesting_refund_boundary_test.go` exercises real staking and bank keepers:
1M vesting coins are delegated and 200k begin unbonding. Delegated vesting remains
1M until maturity. The refund then reduces it to 800k and credits 200k locked
coins. An installed bank send-restriction observer receives no call during the
refund. Real staking/distribution checks pass. This rules out using the existing
send restriction as the sole integration point for retained-stake accounting.

A coordinated-account design must handle this bank/account mutation boundary,
including synchronous refunds and the SDK's free-first counter reduction.
An EndBlock sweep alone cannot make an earlier account mutation atomic with its
counterpart or guarantee correct spendability to intervening operations. Merely
redirecting every future refund from the operator to the original vault is also
insufficient: new stake, key revocation and later address registration must not
route another owner's assets into an old vault. Any refund routing design needs
explicit retained-position ownership and lifecycle rules, without moving the
staking delegation or pending slashability records away from the operator.

Next implementation investigation: an account/bank integration that coordinates
retained obligations during the native mutation, with exact source identity,
rollback and event behavior. Required coverage includes native delegation,
undelegation maturity and immediate refunds, cancellation, slashes, revocation,
re-registration and export/restart. This is not yet implemented; the preflight
refusal remains in effect.

## Shared address mapping

`BuildVaultAddressMigrationPlan` reads the current player-address index, resolves
each player through CurrentContext and derives its vault address. All registered
addresses are included, including secondary keys. Multiple addresses can map to
one vault. Unregistered bank accounts are not selected. Orphaned player references,
blocked accounts and indexed protocol vaults fail preflight. The function neither
provisions accounts nor moves funds, and sorts movements by source address.

All asset phases must consume the same mapping. Infusion.PlayerId is not an
ownership source. A change in address registration can make that field stale.

## SDK records that must move together

| State | Inspected SDK operations | Required handling |
|---|---|---|
| Delegation shares | `GetDelegation`, `SetDelegation`, `RemoveDelegation` | Merge shares at destination; conserve validator total shares. Coordinate distribution hooks and defer Structs reconciliation until nested hook commits finish. |
| Unbonding record and validator index | `SetUnbondingDelegation`, `RemoveUnbondingDelegation` | Merge entry lists without losing individual operation IDs, creation heights, completion times, balances or holds. |
| Redelegation record and source/destination validator indices | `SetRedelegation`, `RemoveRedelegation` | Move every source/destination pair, preserving shares destination, initial balances and all entries. The new delegator must also own the destination delegation that slashing resolves. |
| Unbonding operation-ID index | `SetUnbondingDelegationByUnbondingID` | Rewrite every preserved entry ID to its new record key. This setter also writes the operation type. |
| Redelegation operation-ID index | `SetRedelegationByUnbondingID` | Rewrite every preserved entry ID to its new record key and preserve its operation type. |
| Unbonding maturity queue | `GetUBDQueueTimeSlice`, `SetUBDQueueTimeSlice` | Rewrite the delegator in each DVPair at the same completion timestamp. Preserve unrelated queue members. |
| Redelegation maturity queue | `GetRedelegationQueueTimeSlice`, `SetRedelegationQueueTimeSlice` | Rewrite the delegator in each DVVTriplet at the same completion timestamp. Preserve unrelated queue members. |

The SDK record-removal methods remove the record and validator indices. They do
not rewrite the maturity queue or operation-ID indices. Treating removal followed
by a record setter as a complete transfer would leave dangling references.

Do not call `SetUnbondingDelegationEntry` to recreate an existing entry: it calls
`IncrementUnbondingID` and can invoke `AfterUnbondingInitiated`. An existing operation
must keep its identity and hold state. Similarly, do not start a fresh redelegation
as a substitute for moving an in-flight record.

## Implemented unbonding phase

`MigrateVaultUnbondingRecords` is implemented but not registered in the upgrade
handler. It requires provisioned destinations, checks operation-ID references
before mutations, merges every entry unchanged, and rewrites the records,
validator indices, ID indices and existing queue tuples. It does not use an
entry-creation API, move bank tokens or reconcile infusions.

The real-app regression merges two signing addresses and an occupied vault,
checks preserved entries and ID resolution, rejects a mismatched ID before source
removal, and then exercises SDK slashing, maturity payout and held-operation
release. An unregistered owner's record is unchanged. Rerunning the phase does
not duplicate the merged entries. The full Go suite passes.

The phase now deduplicates references to an affected merged record within each
completion timestamp, keeping its first queue position. `CompleteUnbonding` scans
all entries in the record and handles every mature, unheld entry, so repeated
references would only repeat that scan. Unrelated queue tuples retain order and
multiplicity. The test calls SDK maturity-queue dequeue, verifies one scheduled
vault record, completes its eligible entries, then releases the held operation
by its unchanged ID after the time slice has been removed.

This does not yet prove complete EndBlock throughput or chain export/restart.
Distinct timestamps can still trigger repeated scans of a large merged record;
per-timestamp deduplication does not establish a bound on that total work. This
phase must be coordinated with the remaining asset migrations.

### Self-delegation integration boundary (SDK v0.53.5 review)

Moving the operator's delegation bytes is insufficient. The pinned SDK has two
separate enforcement paths that must agree on the new self-stake identity:

- `x/staking/keeper/delegation.go`'s `Unbond` compares raw delegator bytes with
  raw operator bytes before jailing for a balance below `MinSelfDelegation`.
  This also runs during redelegation. A vault address fails that equality, so
  changing only Structs transaction authorization would bypass the minimum.
- `x/slashing/keeper/unjail.go`'s `Unjail` loads the delegation at the operator
  address and refuses when it is absent. Merely migrating the stake would leave
  a jailed validator unable to unjail even with sufficient vault-held stake.
- `x/staking/keeper/msg_server.go`'s `EditValidator` validates an increased
  minimum against the validator's total tokens. It does not resolve a new
  self-delegation address. Do not treat an accepted edit as proof that the
  operator vault meets the minimum.

If vault self-delegation is selected, enforce it across native staking and
slashing messages as well as Structs reactor messages. An outer keeper wrapper
alone does not replace `Unbond`'s internal address comparison. A hook-based
solution must account for full removal firing before deletion and partial
reduction firing after the updated delegation is stored; otherwise it repeats
the stale-share bug already repaired on branch 118b. The operator-to-player
binding also needs explicit behavior when a signing address is revoked or
registered to a different player: a stale cached owner cannot authorize or
attribute self-stake.

Required real-app evidence includes partial/full undelegation and redelegation
below the minimum, restoration followed by unjailing, unchanged tombstone and
jail-duration checks, native versus Structs entry points, and operator address
ownership changes. Until these paths agree, self-delegation preflight refusal
remains an implementation blocker, not permission to omit those assets.

Further SDK inspection rules out simply jailing inside the partial/full-removal
hooks: `Unbond` retains a validator value from before those hooks and later passes
it to `RemoveValidatorTokensAndShares`, which writes that value back and rebuilds
its power index. The SDK's own minimum-self-delegation jail explicitly reloads
the validator before this step; a jail added to a later hook does not receive
that reload. `TestSDKCapturedValidatorWriteCanUndoHookJail` reproduces the write
boundary with real staking: a stored jail flag is overwritten by the captured
validator even when removing zero shares to isolate the effect from token movement.
It does not inject a replacement hook chain or implement vault self-delegation.

A vault-aware implementation therefore needs an integration point that changes
the self-stake identity check before the SDK's own jail/reload sequence, or a
fully reviewed equivalent covering all callers after the final write. Updating
only Structs handlers would leave native undelegation/redelegation uncovered;
updating only slashing's unjail lookup would leave minimum enforcement uncovered.
Do not waive the minimum or duplicate the moved shares to make migration pass.

## Merge and continuation proof obligations

### Post-upgrade signing-address assets

The stable vault and the assets still held by signing addresses are separate
continuation cases. Current `AddressRegister` moves an incoming address's
transferable delegations and spendable coins to the player's primary address.
`AddressRevoke` does the same for the revoked address, leaving blocked stake
bonded and disowning its infusion so recovery cannot be held hostage.
`PlayerUpdatePrimaryAddress` moves the old primary's transferable assets to the
new primary. These paths have not been redirected to vaults. Ordinary bank
receipts at a signing address are also not automatically swept into its vault.

Decide and implement the intended compatibility/routing behavior before calling
the post-upgrade asset lifecycle complete. A redirect must preserve reward
settlement order, fee-funding behavior, vesting restrictions and the existing
strict-versus-disown recovery policy; invoking the whole-chain upgrade migration
from an address transaction is not a bounded replacement.

`TestPlayerKeyRotationPreservesVaultStakeAndRevokesAccess` independently verifies
the already-vaulted case with real staking: primary rotation and old-key
revocation preserve the vault account number, owner, balance and delegation.
The revoked key cannot spend from it, while the new primary can continue
infusing. This does not claim that later signing-address assets are swept to
vaults or that blocked/vesting registration cases have been redesigned.

### Vesting representation investigation

`vault_vesting_schedule_test.go` contains executable SDK counterexamples, not a
completed migration test. They rule out replacing several vesting accounts with
one ordinary SDK vesting account whose amounts/counters are summed:

- Two delayed schedules each hold 100 ualpha. The first is fully delegated and
  ends at time 100; the second is liquid and ends at time 200. At time 150 the
  original accounts still lock 100. Aggregated delegated-vesting counters reduce
  the aggregate lock to zero, prematurely releasing the second schedule's funds.
  Initial and final balance checks alone miss this interval.
- Two identical continuous schedules each vest 100 over three seconds. The SDK
  rounds each independently: their combined vested amounts are 66 then 134.
  One combined 200-coin schedule produces 67 then 133. Even matching endpoints
  and dates cannot justify merging the schedules.

The next implementation needs a vault vesting account that retains independent
schedule/counter components, preserving the sum of original `LockedCoins(t)`
before subsequent owner actions. Preserve continuous, delayed, periodic and
permanent-lock semantics without approximating continuous schedules as a huge
list of per-second periods. SDK `TrackDelegation` consumes vesting amounts first;
`TrackUndelegation` consumes delegated-free counters first. A composite account
must define deterministic component allocation while preserving those aggregate
priorities, and must carry the counters associated with pending unbondings.

That account needs codec/genesis registration, exact export/restart coverage,
bank/staking continuation tests and migration events. Its per-operation cost
must be measured against the maximum component count in the migration input;
per-account scans cannot be assumed cheap merely because aggregation occurs once.
The existing vesting preflight refusal remains until this representation and the
coordinated account/balance/staking migration are implemented. It is not an
approved exclusion from the migration.

The unwired `MigrateVaultReactorInfusions` phase now rebuilds player-vault

### Release blocker: schedule interface unpack budget

The initial composite uses one protobuf `Any` per source schedule. SDK v0.53.5
limits nested interface unpack calls to 100. The new
`TestVaultVestingAnySchedulesExceedSDKUnpackBudget` demonstrates that an account
with 101 source schedules successfully marshals but cannot unmarshal as an auth
account (`call limit exceeded`). This is a counterexample test, not successful
large-account coverage. The migration must not be activated with this format.

Replace per-source `Any` with concrete schedule data: a validated schedule kind,
source address, original/delegated coin counters, start/end times and periodic
vesting periods. Reconstruct the four supported SDK schedule implementations
for their exact arithmetic. Preserve signing keys/account numbers/sequences on
the converted source BaseAccounts; historical signing keys need not be embedded
in vault schedules. The vault's account interface remains registered normally.
Do not raise the global SDK unpack budget or exclude excess source schedules.
After the change, replace the counterexample with successful large-account
binary/JSON and real auth/restart coverage and measure per-operation cost.

The format blocker above is now repaired in the unreleased schema.
`VaultVestingSchedule` stores concrete kind, source address, times, coin counters
and periodic vesting periods, with no nested `Any`. Decoding reconstructs the
supported SDK schedule implementation to retain its arithmetic; unknown kinds,
irrelevant schedule fields and invalid coin counters are rejected. Original
source signing identities remain in their converted BaseAccounts.

The former counterexample has been replaced by
`TestVaultVestingManySchedulesSurviveSDKUnpackBudget`: 200 schedules round-trip
through binary and JSON AccountI/genesis codecs, then delegate and refund funds
and round-trip again with exact locks. The full Go suite, including real bank,
migration, export/restart and upgrade-block events, passes with the new format.
Large real-app restart and per-operation cost measurement remain outstanding;
passing the codec test is not a production-size performance bound.

### Schedule cost measurements

`BenchmarkVaultVestingSchedules` measures auth decode, locked-balance calculation
and a delegate/refund pair independently of store gas. A local 100 ms sample
per case on 2026-09-10 produced the following approximate results for delayed
schedules containing one denomination:

| Schedules | Auth bytes | Decode | Locked balance | Delegate + refund |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 100 | 6,995 | 49 µs | 173 µs | 458 µs |
| 1,000 | 69,096 | 458 µs | 1.79 ms | 4.64 ms |
| 10,000 | 690,096 | 4.60 ms | 17.97 ms | 48.48 ms |

The 10,000-schedule pair allocates approximately 16.7 MB. These are local short
benchmark samples, not validator throughput guarantees. Reproduce with
`go test -mod=readonly ./x/structs/types -run '^$' -bench BenchmarkVaultVestingSchedules -benchtime=100ms -count=1`.
Costs are approximately linear here, but periodic schedules and multiple
denominations add work. Auth account rewrites also charge store gas against
the complete encoded account; the CPU benchmark does not prove that a free
Structs transaction fits its 20M cap. Real-app metered operation measurements
and the actual migration's maximum source/schedule cardinalities remain required
before selecting the final representation or enabling migration.

Real-app handler gas is now measured by
`TestVaultVestingInfusionGasByScheduleCount`. With Bech32 source strings, 10,000
single-denomination delayed schedules consumed 28,592,442 gas and exceeded the
20M free-message meter. Source addresses now use canonical bytes in the
unreleased schedule schema, reducing that fixture from 730,057 to 470,057 auth
payload bytes and to 18,452,442 handler gas. The 100- and 1,000-schedule fixtures
consume 305,637 and 1,955,409 gas respectively. The test now enforces that these
simple fixtures fit the handler cap and verifies discarded executions preserve
the parent counters.

This does not establish a universal 10,000-schedule allowance: ante overhead is
excluded, and more denominations, periods or different operations cost more.
The original CPU benchmark table predates the byte-address optimization.
Full transaction gas and actual migration cardinalities remain release gates.
In JSON, each schedule's `sourceAddress` is now base64-encoded canonical bytes;
the migration event's separate `sourceAddresses` list remains Bech32 text for
identifying converted auth accounts.

The gas test now also signs and delivers ReactorInfuse through FinalizeBlock,
including the actual ante chain and free-message gas router. The 100-, 1,000-
and 10,000-schedule fixtures use 357,078, 2,006,850 and 18,503,883 total transaction
gas respectively and update the expected delegated-vesting and locked balances.
These are simple delayed schedules with one denomination and a single infusion;
they do not establish bounds for other schedule shapes, batches or transaction
types. Actual migration cardinalities and broader worst-case gas remain open.

## Wide merged pending records (2026-09-21)

Sixteen-address and 1,024-address owner merges now preserve every pending entry and
its ID, support partial/full cancellation and both native slash paths, refund the
full remaining amounts at maturity, and allow owner-authorized defusion afterward.
New requests respect the native SDK entry limit: a merged record above `MaxEntries`
blocks new defusion until enough entries complete. Entries are never discarded to
make room. Same-height cancellation follows the SDK's first matching entry; other
entries remain unchanged in both partial and full cancellation previews.

Accounting tests exposed quadratic pending-record writes in both native unbonding
and destination-unbonding redelegation slashing. Both are now patched in the pinned
repository-local SDK selected by `go.mod`. Four destination scenarios, nine direct
eligibility cases and real hook-visibility assertions pass. Restoring the original
SDK fails resource guards; flushing only after hooks fails the Defusing assertion.
The remaining repeated reads in active-stake hooks are now removed by an immutable,
pair-specific summary scoped to each `Unbond` call. Normal resource guards cover
1,024 addresses, a 4,096-address diagnostic is approximately linear, and a raw-store
hook observer independently catches delayed writes. No summary is persisted.
Broader combined inventory and final native continuation remain open. See the
[resource record](v0.22.0-economy-performance.md#production-sdk-integration-and-hook-visibility)
and [SDK maintenance](../../third_party/README.md).
