## Original failure that changed the implementation order

`TestAgreementRevenueExcludesQueuedReactorOutage` now includes native staking,
real bank payments and actual expiry before the delayed funding infusion is
processed. Its two-block agreement posts 200,000 ualpha: capacity 50,000,
rate 2, and a 50% provider cancellation holdback.

| Case | Original observed outcome | Required outcome under the working policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Healthy for both blocks | 100,000 withdrawable before expiry | Same |
| One reactor jailed, sufficient independent primary power | 100,000 withdrawable; normal expiry releases the other 100,000 | Same; a root transition alone is not a service failure |
| Service lost at height 2; expiry at height 3 | Consumer gets zero; provider can withdraw all 200,000 | Consumer gets 150,000; provider keeps 50,000 for the one served block |
| Recovery before queued cleanup | The unavailable block still becomes provider earnings | Recovery cannot establish that the earlier block was served |

The expiry bucket is visited at exactly its scheduled height. `Expire` removes
the allocation and agreement, decrements load, and releases the holdback before
the later EndBlock power worker catches up. The existing solvency invariant still
passes after the failed refund: deleting the agreement also deletes the nominal
obligation it measures. Log: `/tmp/structs-agreement-outage-expiry.log`.

This means a late correction inside allocation destruction alone cannot fix the
problem. By then expiry may have removed the agreement and the provider may have
withdrawn the money. Simply returning an error from `Checkpoint` is also unsafe:
expiry has no retry, and leaving its load active recreates the inherited
cross-consumer collateral drain.

The player-facing corpus describes upfront escrow, elapsed-block revenue and
expiry conversion to earnings in its [energy-market reference](https://github.com/playstructs/structs-ai/blob/61915c8cba8d2dfd68ae8a930bcafba2bfe1deca/knowledge/economy/energy-market.md).
It does not specify delayed outage settlement. Repository behavior remains the
source of truth; changing financial finality or withdrawal availability requires
an explicit client/operator contract and an upstream documentation update.

## Current-state evidence must have three outcomes

The allocation ancestry check formerly returned one boolean for both a completed
shortage check and exhaustion of its 2,048-unit allowance. That boolean cannot
serve as a billing oracle: an unvisited part of a healthy graph is not evidence
that a service interval failed.

`CurrentContext.CheckSubstationSupply` now distinguishes supported, unsupported
and incomplete checks. The existing readiness/admission wrapper still accepts
only supported supply. Incomplete owner inventories, unresolved incoming index
rows, arithmetic inconsistencies and exhausted traversal budgets remain closed
for admission, while preserving their distinction from a completed refusal.
Tests cover exact inventory/depth boundaries, an actual cached capacity shortage,
recovery in the same context, a visited cycle and a stale index entry.

This is current-state evidence only. A supported result does not certify past
service, an unsupported result does not supply its first occurrence height, and
no result authorizes moving collateral. The owner projection remains a discount
of stored capacity, not proof of the origin of every stored unit.

## Proposed separation of resource retirement and financial settlement

The following is an implementation direction to validate, not a schema decision.

1. **Preserve an unsettled obligation before retirement.** Expiry or teardown
   must release allocation/provider load exactly once at the scheduled operation,
   even if service verification is unfinished. A durable obligation must retain
   the consumer owner, provider/pool, denom, rate, penalty terms, capacity/window,
   prior certified payouts and unresolved collateral. Deleting an agreement or
   provider cannot delete the last way to resolve or pay that obligation.
2. **Reserve uncertain collateral.** A shared pool must keep the unresolved
   consumer claim funded. A revenue clamp against the bank balance alone does
   not reserve other consumers' money. Already certified earnings can remain
   withdrawable; an incomplete check must not mark additional blocks as earned.
3. **Advance accounting from evidence.** Provider aggregate load times elapsed
   blocks cannot price agreements with different verified cutoffs. Settlement
   needs per-obligation progress and a conserved cumulative payout ledger, with
   aggregate counters updated from actual deltas. Rounding and repeated small
   checkpoints must conserve the same principal as a single settlement.
4. **Process bounded work with durable retries.** Financial work may outlive
   physical expiry. Queue visitation, historical reads, graph edges, inventory
   rows and cleanup must have explicit shared allowances and rotation. A failed
   item retains both its reserved funds and retry state; an empty expiry bucket
   must not be the only future trigger.

Capacity/duration changes must settle or preserve the old economic window before
rebasing it. They cannot advance the provider clock past unverified old load and
then apply new load retrospectively. Consumer/provider cancellation must retain
their existing priority and distinguish a preceding supply failure from a
voluntary exit. Revoked signing keys cannot redirect an existing consumer claim;
vault owner IDs, rather than a stale signer address, identify its beneficiary.

## Historical evidence and resource bounds remain unresolved

A single reactor outage height is insufficient. Independent supply can keep an
owner supported, and later top-ups, slashes, ownership changes, generator changes,
allocation edits and recovery can erase what a current-state walk would see.
The evidence source must preserve the relevant earlier facts without visiting
every affected player/agreement in a staking hook.

A candidate approach is versioned, destination-scoped facts plus resumable
service verification. Before selecting it, demonstrate all of the following:

- Which writes record facts: topology and power/load changes, infusion weight
  and owner changes, validator/delegation backing, generator lifecycle and live
  address ownership. Intermediate SDK hook state is not a completed block state.
- A precise same-block convention consistent with agreement opening,
  checkpointing and EndBlock ordering; already elapsed blocks cannot be re-priced
  from a later health observation.
- Immutable finite work intervals, correct reconstruction after deletion or
  recovery, and progress under continuing deposits and topology changes.
- Storage/pruning bounds and reference lifetimes. A slow obligation cannot
  silently pin an unlimited global history, nor may pruning discard its evidence.
- An independent-service control at each graph layer. Observing a contributing
  root's outage must not automatically terminate a still-supported agreement.

Do not use optional node-local IAVL history or external indexer events as
consensus evidence: validators may retain different historical versions. An
on-chain representation needs deterministic writes, cached rollback, canonical
commit order, export/import and explicit indexing. The storage primitive below
is implemented and tested. An opt-in canonical-store capture adapter now has
real-app coverage. A resumable, in-memory historical supply evaluator is now
implemented below. Indexed persistent jobs reuse that evaluator. Finite interval jobs now advance
through dependency-change boundaries and retain the first unsupported height;
the retained ledger and bounded EndBlock scheduler below now consume those results.
Retention, migration/genesis activation and the complete financial lifecycle
remain unfinished.

## Implemented journal foundation, not activated

`x/structs/keeper/service_history.go` now supplies a `ServiceHistory` storage
primitive under `Agreement/serviceHistory/`. The application now wires an opt-in
capture adapter, but neither migration nor genesis enables it. Export/import
still does not carry these records, so activation remains prohibited until the
complete historical and financial lifecycle is implemented.

The primitive supports explicit Structs-state, validator and delegation domains,
with binary-safe object scopes and keys. Callers initialize a complete baseline,
record changes and explicitly seal a height after its final supply facts are
captured. Sealed heights cannot be rewritten. Same-block writes coalesce;
unchanged values add no historical version. Even an unchanged observation
advances the scope's capture clock, preventing a later backwards write from
changing what an earlier no-op claimed to observe. Nil means deletion; an empty
non-nil value remains an existing fact.

Point lookup seeks directly to the latest version at/before the requested sealed
height. A lookup before the initialized baseline, beyond the seal or below a
pruned fact's floor returns an error, rather than a usable empty result. A later
large payload is not loaded merely to read a smaller old version. The journal
reads its own retained records from the current KV store; it does not load old
IAVL versions.

Historical enumeration uses an explicit object scope and immutable insertion
ordinals. The per-height creation cutoff excludes facts first introduced after
the requested sealed height. A binary key-prefix scan was rejected: later keys
could otherwise extend a historical job indefinitely. Each batch consumes at
most 128 insertion entries, including tombstones, and refuses missing/gapped or
mismatched index entries. A 130-fact snapshot finishes in two calls despite
1,024 subsequent arrivals before the second call. Recovery and recreation retain
the older values for the same captured interval.

Pruning removes at most 128 versions per call while retaining the latest version
at/before the caller-provided floor as a reconstruction anchor. Queries below the
floor refuse even while a partial prune still leaves some old bytes present.
The primitive does not choose the floor or retire scope indexes. Pending-claim
references, safe complete-scope reclamation and aggregate storage limits remain
required before activation.

Tests use the real SDK KV store and nested `CacheContext` behavior: an inner
commit followed by outer rollback discards facts, scope counters, seals and
pruning together. Committing and reopening the latest multistore preserves old
facts. This is storage/reload coverage, not app genesis export coverage or proof
that every native staking lifecycle is covered. The additional real-app capture
coverage below exercises actual keeper writes.

The pinned SDK prefix/cache iterator `Error()` reports normal exhaustion as an
invalid iterator. The implementation follows their `Valid()` termination contract,
closes iterators before writes, and validates stored keys and values explicitly.
The initial tests exposed that SDK-specific behavior rather than suppressing a
failed financial assertion.

## Opt-in canonical-write capture

`app/service_capture_store.go` wraps the store service supplied to Structs and
native staking. Reads remain ordinary SDK reads. Selected writes first check
`Agreement/serviceCaptureEnabled`; the flag remains absent in normal genesis
and the current upgrade. Once enabled, each selected canonical Set/Delete and
its history update share a child SDK cache and the caller's gas meter. Capture
errors refuse the canonical write, including when a caller ignores the error.
Outer transaction/upgrade rollback discards both sides. This is below
CurrentContext commits, so it does not add a second entity cache or re-enter
staking hooks.

The SDK's core v0.11.3 store service is an unnamed interface alias. Binding its
source spelling in depinject silently left the ordinary service installed.
The adapter derives the actual reflected interface name and uses a module-scoped
provider. Tests invoke the real app keepers and compare canonical bytes with
journal facts, so an unused or wrongly scoped adapter cannot pass them.

`InitializeServiceHistoryCapture` explicitly seeds selected canonical prefixes
and indexes in one SDK cache before setting the enable flag. It refuses an
already-enabled journal, a nonempty partial baseline, negative heights and
malformed scoped index keys. The empty allocation-destination bucket is
explicitly excluded: it names no supply node, and connecting an unassigned
allocation normally deletes from it. Its allocation record and real destination
index remain captured. Failed baseline seeding leaves no flag or journal;
a corrected retry can initialize normally. This is an upgrade/import operation,
never an on-demand transaction scan. Production baseline time, storage and cache
memory remain unmeasured.

| Current supply input | Captured canonical state |
| --- | --- |
| Allocation ancestry and power/load | Allocation records, destination-scoped allocation index, grid attributes; substation records are retained for subsequent historical checks |
| Owner infusion inventory and backing | Owner-scoped infusion index, infusion records including weights/commission, native delegation and validator records |
| Live ownership resolution | Address/player associations, vault reverse associations, player and guild records |
| Generator lifecycle | Struct record, struct attributes and struct type |
| Reactor source gate | Reactor record, efficiency-active flag, commission revision, native validator record |

This maps the current `CheckSubstationSupply`, owner-capacity projection,
`generatorInfusionEligible` and `reactorSourceEligible` reads. It is not a proof
that a future historical evaluator has no additional inputs. Operational queues,
permissions and account counters are not substituted for supply facts. Scoped
index creation, re-homing and deletion are observed at their canonical writes.

The Structs EndBlocker seals only after its final supply mutation phase. An
actual FinalizeBlock/Commit test also requires Structs to remain the last
configured EndBlock module. Each seal represents final state at height H;
intermediate hook values in the same block coalesce. Integrating the financial
service interval convention remains required before activating billing readers.

## Historical supply evaluation, not financial settlement

`service_supply_view.go` reconstructs every mutable supply input from the same
sealed height. The view has a journal, height and codec; it cannot fall through
to a live keeper read. It decodes grid values, topology, owner inventories,
address/vault ownership, generator records/status/types, reactor revisions,
validators and delegations. Invalid encodings, unresolved indexed rows and
pruned evidence return errors. They do not become zero power or a completed
outage classification. Native reactor power arithmetic is shared with live
admission through `projectReactorInfusionPower`.

`NewServiceSupplyProof` and `AdvanceServiceSupplyProof` provide an internal
continuation over that view. Each advance processes at most 128 node/index steps;
owner inventories and allocation ancestry share that allowance. A step has a
fixed set of point reads and at most one historical inventory entry. Tombstones
consume work. The traversal distinguishes an active ancestor cycle from a
completed shared ancestor and preserves intermediate counters/cursors on a
failed read so the same step remains pending. New entries after the selected
height cannot extend its finite inventory.

A supported substation ancestry check is not a contract's delivery proof: an
empty substation can have a valid zero-load graph while supplying no purchased
capacity. `NewAllocationSupplyProof` also resolves the allocation at the same
height, checks its source against the caller's preserved contract source,
requires at least the requested power, and checks that the source load includes
it. Missing, moved or insufficient entitlements are unsupported; malformed
records remain errors. An allocation need not have a destination to represent
reserved capacity—the consumer may choose when to connect it. The amount and
source must eventually come from the preserved agreement window, never from
current provider fields or a user-supplied claim.

The evaluator preserves the current ancestry policy: every positive contributing
allocation must have a supported source. It does not introduce a new policy for
redistributing a failed branch's load across healthy branches. Owner projection
still discounts stored capacity; it does not independently prove the origin of
every raw capacity unit. Those limits must remain explicit in the final economic
invariants and service contract.

Real-app tests demonstrate these reconstruction properties:

- A native jail/validator-set departure is unsupported at its sealed height after
  recovery and after later topology destruction. Sufficient independent primary
  backing remains supported at that same height. A proof begun before recovery
  resumes over the earlier facts.
- Generator shutdown/restoration and later reassignment of a legacy infusion
  address preserve their earlier supported/unsupported results.
- A native slash preserves a guild vault's sufficient remaining backing while
  reactor commission stays gated at the historical unverified revision. Later
  repair cannot replace either earlier result. Pruning required validator facts
  returns an error and leaves the proof incomplete on repeated attempts.
- A 1,200-link synthetic graph completes in 3,601 steps over 29 calls, shared
  ancestors succeed, cycles are refused, and an unresolved index remains pending.
  This synthetic legacy-mode graph tests traversal, not native backing origin.
- A 130-entry tombstone inventory finishes in two calls despite later arrivals.
  Its final four steps cost 27,321 SDK gas with either 32 or 4,096 later entries.
- An empty graph does not prove an allocation entitlement; a later allocation
  cannot establish earlier delivery, and requesting more than its assigned power
  fails. Capture permits creating and connecting real unassigned allocations.

The last case exposed a capture compatibility bug: the old mapper rejected the
normal deletion from `Allocation/destination//<id>`. It now ignores that
no-object index bucket, including legacy baseline rows, while still capturing
the allocation and its actual destination index. The regression goes through
`NewAllocation` and `SetDestination` with capture enabled.

`ServiceSupplyProof` itself remains an in-memory reference continuation. Its
stack/visited memory grows with the graph. The persistent implementation below
uses separately indexed frames/visited records and the same single-step
interpreter. Neither form is called by a production transaction or block worker.
Retention references, queue fairness, byte/gas bounds and export/import remain
required. A single-height result also
is not an interval certificate: earliest loss and certified cumulative earnings
must be established over the preserved service window. No result here moves
collateral, changes a checkpoint, pays a provider or retires a financial claim.
Capture remains disabled in normal migration/genesis.

## Indexed persistent verification jobs

`service_supply.proto` defines compact job metadata and individual frame records;
Go and Pulsar bindings are generated. Result/phase zero values are explicitly
unspecified and are refused, as are unknown enum numbers. Schema version, job
identity, source/entitlement, exclusive evidence bounds, pending stack depth and
completed zero depth are validated when a header is read. The private supply-job
schema is now version 2; earlier prototype headers are refused. Frames validate their phase and object kind;
a missing frame or invalid ancestor state cannot count as completed work.

`service_supply_job.go` stores jobs under `Agreement/serviceVerification/`.
A length-prefixed internal job id separates its header, depth-keyed stack frames
and object-keyed ancestor rows. Prefix-like ids and embedded zero bytes cannot
alias another job. `CreateServiceSupplyJob` binds an id to one historical height,
allocation, source and required power. Repeating that tuple returns existing
progress; a changed tuple is refused.

`AdvanceServiceSupplyJob` reads one frame and the necessary indexed ancestor
state per step. It invokes the same `nextServiceSupplyStep` used by the in-memory
reference and writes only the frames/ancestor entries changed by this pass.
It never loads or serializes the entire previous traversal. Up to 128 steps
share one private SDK cache. An ordinary evidence error commits the valid prefix
of the pass, leaves the failing step pending and returns its error; the failed
attempt still counts against returned work. A repeated error starts at that step.
The caller's outer SDK rollback discards the whole pass, and a gas panic before
its private cache commits leaves all prior job bytes unchanged.

Completed jobs retain their result header until the caller consumes/preserves it
in the financial ledger. Cleanup refuses pending jobs and removes at most 128
rows per call, including the final header. It handles both successful traversals
whose frames have already been popped and an unsupported result leaving a large
active stack. Cleanup is not payout replay protection: the future obligation
ledger must separately record paid/refunded amounts and govern job-id reuse.

The earlier checkpoint in `/tmp/structs-service-job-target.log` verified these
cases. Its gas measurements below predate the compact change index; current
measurements are in the following interval section:

- A partial job survives actual FinalizeBlock/Commit and a new application
  instance opened on the committed database. The new keeper finishes from stored
  frames without a Go continuation or optional old IAVL version, matches the
  reference interpreter's total steps, and commits continued blocks.
- Nested creation, advancement and cleanup all respect outer SDK rollback.
  Repeated creation preserves progress, changed entitlement terms are rejected,
  and repeated advancement of a completed job does no work.
- A stale historical index retains the valid prefix, then retries only the
  failing step. Unspecified/unknown results and phases, plus missing frames,
  cannot advance or silently reset the job.
- Native outage/recovery and sufficient independent backing retain their earlier
  results through durable progress and later topology destruction.
- Cleanup remains bounded for both completion directions and cannot erase
  neighbouring jobs whose ids share prefixes or contain zero bytes.
- The first 128-step graph pass costs 4,176,130 SDK gas with either 130 or 1,200
  links. Early and late out-of-gas interruptions preserve the complete previous
  job state. This synthetic graph cost is not a worst-case native infusion cost.

The separate accumulated-state test in
`/tmp/structs-service-job-accumulation.log` measures 4,193,542 gas before any prior
work and 4,193,584 after 1,024 earlier steps of the same 1,200-link graph. Only
bounded metadata encoding differs; the already-stored stack/ancestor inventory

an agreement by itself.

## Finite interval verification at dependency changes

`service_history_span.go` adds exclusive bounds to point and scoped reads.
A point read returns its value at H and the next recorded change before the
caller's sealed end. An absent fact is bounded by its first creation. A scope
batch intersects the next insertion height with the next changes of its visited
rows, including tombstones. Empty/exhausted batches still see future insertions.
A proof must consume the whole required inventory; one batch's bound does not
certify unvisited rows. Negative reads, ownership, native backing and topology
all use this same mechanism. Unrelated objects do not shorten a proof's bound.

The first cost experiment exposed a GasKV iterator detail: a key-only seek into
payload versions still charges the future value. A 1 MiB future value raised the
measured read from 8,056 to 3,153,781 gas. `RecordInScope` now writes a one-byte
change marker beside every payload version under `c/factID/height`; pruning
removes both under the same version key and retains both anchor rows. Read paths
refuse missing head/selected-version change markers. Boundary seeks use this
compact index instead of the payload store. This adds one small write per
material version and index-validation reads; it is not free capture overhead.
A boundary lookup now costs 10,317 gas in both the small fixture and a fixture
with a 1 MiB future payload plus 4,096 unrelated changes. No global change-height
scan or per-block scan is needed.

`CreateServiceSupplySpanJob` binds a proof to a fixed [height,end) ceiling, with
end-1 already sealed. Every consulted fact intersects `ValidUntil`; only a
completed supported result certifies [height,ValidUntil). The point-job API is
the one-block specialization. A new future seal cannot extend the ceiling, and
an ordinary failed step cannot persist its speculative bound. A boundary is a
reason to recheck, not proof of failure: a validator can change while independent
backing keeps the agreement supplied.

`service_interval_job.go` now combines these spans into an immutable nonempty
service window. It stores a scalar contiguous `VerifiedUntil` under
`Agreement/serviceInterval/` and owns one child proof in a reserved namespace.
Ready, verification, cleanup and completion are explicit validated enum phases.
It checks the next boundary only after disposing of all previous child rows;
creation, graph traversal and deletion share a maximum 128-unit allowance.
Unchanged blocks can therefore be skipped, and completed spans do not accumulate
another list of proof records. An unsupported check stops at `VerifiedUntil`,
the first failed height after the certified prefix. Later recovery cannot erase
it. A malformed/unavailable fact remains an error and pending work, never an
outage. Public standalone-proof APIs refuse the interval-child namespace.

Real-app interval tests cover a recovered outage, independent native supply,
fixed ceilings under 1,026 later index arrivals, actual database reopen and
continued blocks, ordinary error retry, unknown/unspecified phases and results,
SDK outer rollback, gas rollback and shared-budget child cleanup. In a 130-link
fixture, one million healthy blocks require 524 work units; an outage at 40 and
recovery at 60 requires 529 and retains first loss 40. Native jail at height 2
produces first loss 2 without independent backing, while the backed control
survives until topology destruction at height 4.

A 128-unit interval graph pass costs 6,507,132 gas with either 130 or 1,200 links.
A 128-position native owner-inventory pass costs about 12.30 million gas with
130 or 1,026 positions and completes after subsequent bounded passes. The
allocation is admitted before the inventory grows past the live admission
allowance. These fixtures establish selected costs, not maximum block time or
queue throughput. Work across a window still scales with its relevant changes
and graph size; production throughput, retention and combined block cost remain gates.

Validation: `/tmp/structs-service-interval-target.log` passes; the later expanded
checks are in `/tmp/structs-service-interval-final-target.log`. The full suite
`/tmp/structs-service-interval-full-suite.log` completes with only the existing

compatibility against 117b/118b and served OpenAPI regeneration pass.

The bounded EndBlock scheduler below now creates these private evidence jobs

## Retained obligations and real bank settlement

`service_obligation.proto` now defines a durable `ServiceObligation`, one
`ServiceCollateralPool` per provider, and an additive `EventServiceObligation`
replacement snapshot. Corresponding CurrentContext caches deduplicate reads and
commit in sorted order through `CommitAll`. `ReserveServiceObligation` enrolls
only a newly funded agreement at its opening height, requires initialized capture,
checks its allocation/source/power, and reserves its full principal. It refuses
old agreements: migration must allocate their existing earned/paid balances
explicitly instead of pretending they are newly funded.

The obligation freezes consumer owner, provider/pool, allocation/source, currency,
rate, capacity, original window and penalty atomics. It retains a certified
contiguous prefix, first-loss flag, physical retirement reason/height and
cumulative consumer/provider payments after the live agreement is removed.
Each provider reserve tracks both outstanding claims and enrolled active load.
Legacy `Checkpoint` excludes that load, so it cannot bill enrolled agreements
again. Provider revenue sweeps, provider deletion and legacy consumer refunds
respect reserved principal. Provider deletion records the final owner in the
retained pool metadata; subsequent provider payments go directly to that owner's
vault, remaining reachable after the provider record is gone.

Enrolled expiry and cancellation release allocation/load through the existing
shared CurrentContext and retain the financial obligation. They do not require a
history read or bank transfer to retire physical service. Repeated retirement in
a new context is also a no-op. A completed interval must match the frozen
allocation, source, capacity and window before it can extend the certified prefix.
Later recovery cannot clear a recorded loss. Old supported evidence is harmless
on replay; gaps and conflicting loss results are refused.

The financial arithmetic uses integer amounts and 18-place penalty atomics.
Provider accrual is the cumulative floor of served gross times one minus the
provider penalty, with only the difference from `provider_paid` payable.
Successful full-term expiry releases the holdback; consumer cancellation also

intermediate for fractional multiplication; an oversized principal is refused.

## Bounded automatic settlement and capture-gated enrollment

`AgreementOpen` now calls `ReserveServiceObligation` before its single
`CommitAll` whenever historical capture is enabled. New records and their queue
tickets commit through the same sorted CurrentContext as the agreement. This
does not initialize capture or allocate a migration baseline for old agreements.

`ProcessServiceSettlementQueue` runs near the end of EndBlock, before historical
sealing. It visits at most four claims, sharing 128 work units; each visit uses
one coordinator unit and at most 31 other units. FIFO tickets use a monotonic
uint64 counter, a ticket-to-agreement row and an agreement-to-ticket index.
Each pass freezes the last issued ticket. Pending work rotates to a fresh ticket
beyond that cutoff, so neither retries nor new arrivals extend the current pass.
Updating the financial record preserves the task and its queue priority.

The generated task phase explicitly selects assessment/payment, interval
verification, certification or cleanup. A proof freezes its exclusive horizon
at the lesser of the current height and the claim's stop height, using only
earlier sealed history. Later blocks cannot extend an in-flight proof. A proven
loss retires live allocations before final payment. The worker must precede
sealing because that retirement changes the current block's state. Financial
completion can lag expiry or current service by multiple blocks; a withdrawal
cannot release uncertified revenue merely because the latest block has elapsed.

Each step uses a private SDK cache and a fresh CurrentContext. A proof's ordinary
error retains its valid prefix; failed retirement/payment discards that step.
Ordinary task errors retain the reserve and rotate behind earlier claims, allowing
unrelated work to progress. The whole pass has another SDK cache: a gas panic or
outer transaction rollback discards payments, events and rotations together.
Malformed queue structure is reported as an error, not treated as completed
work. There is no automatic reconstruction of corrupted queue state.

The registered `service-settlement-queue` invariant checks unpaid claim
reachability, matching queue/index/task inventories, ticket bounds and uniqueness,
known task phases, and interval ownership/terms. It permits a settled claim to
remain queued until pending cleanup completes. Its full scans run only on demand.
The separate collateral invariant cannot detect a funded claim whose queue entry
has disappeared, which the new corruption tests demonstrate.

## Capacity changes retain separately priced windows

Capacity increases/decreases on enrolled agreements now preserve the elapsed
window and reprice only the remaining capacity-blocks. The current agreement and
claim receive the new capacity, start height and exclusive end. The old window
is retained under `(agreement_id, revision)` with its original capacity,
start/end and cumulative nominal gross before it. The rate and penalties remain
frozen on the claim. A new CurrentContext window cache participates in sorted
CommitAll; no transaction walks every old window.

The remaining duration is `floor(oldRemainingBlocks * oldCapacity / newCapacity)`.
Zero duration, integer overflow, invalid provider ranges, a full expiration
bucket, mismatched allocation/provider source and insufficient current headroom
refuse the change. An increase also requires the existing bounded live-backing
proof, so stale stored capacity cannot authorize more service. Reductions do not
require that unavailable supply become healthy first. Known service failure,
retirement and a short collateral pool cannot be escaped through a rebase.

The total funded principal is unchanged by a capacity change. Any integer
remainder that cannot buy a whole block becomes cumulative `refund_credit` owed
to the consumer. A later change cannot spend that credit. No transfer occurs in
the capacity handler: the settlement worker pays the credit with consumer
priority. Thus update authority is required, while a token-transfer bit is not
required to rearrange this already-funded service. Paid duration extension still
requires both permissions and adds actual new collateral.

The claim now separates current terms from earlier verification. `revision`
counts closed windows, `verified_revision` selects the next one to prove, and
`origin_height` preserves the original start. `verified_until` can precede the
current window's start; it must never be clamped to the new start. Nominal and
certified gross are separate canonical integer totals:

- `prior_window_gross` is the nominal price of all closed windows.
- `certified_gross` is the price of the contiguous proven service prefix, at each
  window's original capacity.
- `released_gross` is the certified gross of completed closed windows whose
  provider holdback is no longer contingent on the new window. It is an
  entitlement basis, not a payment counter.
- `refund_credit` is the unconditional cumulative consumer rounding credit.

Writing `P` for principal, `R` for refund credit, `G` for certified gross and `L`
for released gross, pending provider entitlement is
`L + floor((G - L) * (1 - providerPenalty))`; pending consumer entitlement is
`R`. A proven loss retains that provider entitlement and returns its exact
complement `P - providerEntitlement` to the consumer. Healthy expiry releases
`P - R` to the provider. Healthy consumer cancellation gives the provider
`G + floor((P - R - G) * consumerPenalty)`, with the exact complement refunded.
All payments subtract the previously paid cumulative amounts. Earlier failure
therefore overrides later voluntary cancellation or capacity changes, without
reclaiming valid revenue from completed earlier windows.

Each proof remains inside one frozen window. A capacity change does not rewrite
its interval, visited graph or FIFO ticket. The worker certifies a completed
closed window before releasing its holdback and advances to the next indexed
window in one coordinator unit. A missing or inconsistent window remains an
error with its collateral reserved. Same-block changes collapse current terms;
only an initial change at the opening height may create one empty old window.
The worker advances that window without creating an invalid empty proof.

The collateral invariant now verifies the full closed-window inventory,
contiguity, revision count, nominal gross and the relationship to current terms.
It walks actual rows rather than looping up to an untrusted revision count.
Each loaded revised claim also checks its certified/released totals against
one indexed verification window. Corruption tests remove or alter a window
already behind the verification cursor and verify that the full invariant
still detects it. These complete scans remain on demand, outside handlers and
EndBlock. Closed-window reclamation is not implemented yet; export/import,
retention and bounded cleanup remain release requirements.

## Legacy financial baseline implementation

`Keeper.InitializeServiceAccounting` now prepares the financial baseline and
initial supply journal inside one disposable SDK cache. This helper is not yet
called by the release upgrade or genesis paths: preserving the journal, pending
proofs and obligations through both genesis modes remains required before that
activation. Database reopen and genesis export/import are different operations.

Legacy pools have no per-agreement paid history. Applying nominal earnings per
agreement would invent that history, and even its rounding would disagree with
the old aggregate checkpoint. At upgrade height `H`, each live agreement retains:

```
future = rate * capacity * (oldEnd - H)
held   = floor(rate * capacity * (H - oldStart) * providerPenalty)
newPrincipal = future + held
```

The new claim starts at `H`, with no certified post-upgrade service or payments.
Its `legacy_baseline` receipt preserves `H`, original start/end/capacity and
`held_provider_penalty`. The public agreement's start becomes `H`; its remaining
end, capacity, allocation and ownership stay associated with the claim. The
receipt does not assert whether any pre-upgrade block had healthy supply.

The initializer verifies provider loads against the complete agreement inventory,
checks terms and funding, sets checkpoint clocks to `H`, then reserves **all**
live consumers before any provider sweep. Contracts already due or overdue expire
through the existing teardown path with those live reserves protected. It then
moves each provider's exact unreserved rate-denomination balance to its earnings
pool, preserving earlier payments and accounting for aggregate rounding or
unassigned coins. Other denominations remain in the pool. A short reserve,
malformed contract, bank refusal or late history-initialization failure aborts
the entire operation. Re-initializing an active or partially populated financial
baseline is refused; an atomically failed attempt can be retried.

The old holdback stays contingent. For a claim with held amount `B`, it is excluded
from post-upgrade certified gross and capacity-window nominal totals. Outage or
provider cancellation returns it to the consumer unless a fully verified capacity
rebase has already released it. Healthy expiry releases it to the provider;
healthy consumer cancellation pays `G + B + floor((P - R - B - G) * cp)`, where
`G` is certified post-upgrade gross and `R` is cumulative capacity rounding credit.
The first completely verified closed capacity window releases `B`; an empty
window created at the baseline height also qualifies because it contains no
unverified post-upgrade service. The receipt's `rebase_released` flag records that
eligibility, independently of actual payment. Copy-on-write updates keep a refused
rebase from modifying another cached copy of the receipt.

For example, a rate-3, capacity-1 agreement covering `[1,8)` with provider penalty
0.4 retains 17 coins at `H=3`: 15 for future service and 2 held from the old span.
The provider's earlier payments plus its unreserved remainder total 4 coins.
Healthy expiry later pays all 17; a loss at block 4 pays 1 more to the provider
and returns 16 to the consumer. A healthy capacity increase at block 5 preserves
old terms, releases a 3-coin rounding credit, and ultimately pays 14 from the
new claim. An outage before that rebase must still return the old holdback.

## Service clock across zero-height restarts

Service history and financial windows now use a monotonic service height:
`serviceHeight = clockOffset + currentBlockHeight`, with checked uint64 addition.
An absent offset means zero, preserving ordinary chain behavior. Public agreement
start/end blocks and expiration indexes continue to use the current chain's block
numbers. A claim's end maps back by subtracting the offset; a historical current
window that began before the restart has public start zero. Its true start stays
on the claim and is never discarded by that clamp.

Canonical capture, history seeding/sealing, settlement horizons, new enrollment,
retirement, capacity changes, extensions and the financial invariant now use that
mapping. Proof IDs, closed windows, certified prefixes, old baseline receipts and
cumulative payments keep their original service heights. Capacity rescaling still
uses remaining physical blocks, while past gross is computed over the immutable
logical window. An extension converts its proposed public end to service height
before pricing the extra duration.

`PrepareServiceClockForZeroHeightExport` requires the exact last sealed block,
valid financial state and a complete enrolled live-agreement inventory. Inside a
private SDK cache it rebases public windows and provider checkpoint clocks, then
sets the offset to the old service height. For example, after export from service
height 100, new block 1 means service height 101. A second zero-height restart
adds the intervening blocks to the existing offset rather than replacing it with
the newer chain's smaller block number. The preparation does not change any
history, claim, financial window, proof, task or settlement ticket.

Export reconstruction is not gameplay at the old sealed height. A context-local
export marker suppresses canonical capture during those export-only rewrites;
it is never persisted and does not affect the source context. The architecture
test confines references to this bypass and clock-rewrite capability to the
explicit preparation functions. The app export and genesis paths are still
unwired: they must serialize the complete service state and capture rebuilt
canonical supply at the next service height before this preparation is activated.

## Complete private service inventory

`ServiceGenesis` now includes the historical journal, optional clock-offset row,
obligations, collateral pools, capacity windows, original settlement tickets and
counter presence, interval jobs, proof headers, remaining stack frames and visited
nodes. Frame depth is explicit because unsupported-proof cleanup can leave only a
suffix of the original stack. The type is additive in `service_genesis.proto`;
root `GenesisState.service` now includes it through the application integration
described below.

`ExportServiceGenesis` reconstructs and compares every private service row and
checks the complete prefix inventory. Unknown rows, malformed keys, missing
dependencies and partial capture state refuse export without repairing source
state. `ImportServiceGenesis` requires an empty destination and uses one private
SDK cache. Financial rows commit through fresh CurrentContext caches, after the
original queue is installed. A genesis-only flag suppresses ordinary enrollment:
settled claims must not be enqueued again and pending tickets must not rotate.

Validation checks canonical capture identities, conservation and queue invariants,
payout owners, interval/child bindings, retained historical spans, frame phases,
active-ancestor relationships, and original scope cursors. Unknown visited states

step counters never determine loop lengths; exhausted counters are refused.
Completed unsupported proofs may retain frames or visits awaiting bounded cleanup.
Restoration is a snapshot and emits financial replacement events without making
bank transfers. Late refusal discards both writes and events.

`ReconcileServiceGenesis` is preparation after all modules reconstruct their

same selected canonical prefixes as initial capture to add new facts. Deleted
objects, grid attributes, index entries or native delegations become tombstones.
New entries append after old insertion ordinals even if their keys sort earlier.
Old versions, creation cutoffs, proof cursors and financial state remain unchanged.

## Historical proof validation before retention

Retention review reproduced a gap in genesis validation: a shape-valid proof
could carry altered frame capacity, load, incoming power, a rewound insertion
cursor, a changed step count or an invented completed-node entry. Import accepted
all six changes. It also accepted a later pruning floor on a historical input
that a pending proof had not reached yet. That input would become an error when
verification resumed, leaving the claim pending. Reproduction failures are in
`/tmp/structs-service-proof-semantics-before.log`; these are malformed-snapshot
cases, not transactions available to a player.

`validateServiceGenesisProofSemantics` now reconstructs each retained proof in a
discarded SDK cache from its original historical allocation entitlement and fixed
horizon. It runs the existing deterministic interpreter to the claimed step and
compares the entire job header, stack, accumulated values and visited-node set.
For completed proofs, bounded cleanup may have removed a prefix of the frame/visit
rows; only the exact remaining suffix is accepted. Altered results, validity
horizons and invented rows are refused. A pending proof is then continued to its
actual result in the discarded cache to verify that its unread dependencies are
still usable. None of this progress is installed or paid out.

A forged large step count does not control an empty counter loop: replay stops
when the actual reachable graph finishes or a real input fails. Work nevertheless
scales with each proof's reachable graph, and repeated proofs may revisit shared
inputs. This is genesis/export validation, not a bounded live operation. An
architecture test checks the complete call path through import/export and refuses
use from a transaction or block hook. The same test prevents production use of
the raw pruning primitive while there is no reference manager to select a safe
floor. No pruning worker has been enabled by this change.

### Retention dependencies and required deletion order

The [retention implementation design](v0.22.0-history-retention-design.md) expands
this matrix into the collection/admission contract, financial and indexer deletion
sequence, and required concurrency/resource tests. It distinguishes the bounded
implemented collection/ownership components and internal terminal cleanup from
production activation and safe pruning, which remain unfinished.

The current code requires the following references to be handled explicitly:

| Retained state | Evidence or metadata that must remain available | Release condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unsettled obligation | Every unverified service interval through its funded stop, including windows no current proof reached; import/export now replay the sealed portion through the first outage to check that future worker reads are usable | Certification advances the obligation; no active proof alone describes all future verification work. Never require blocks beyond the current seal to exist at import |
| Certified claim prefix (live or settled) | Original service origin through the certified frontier, each window's original capacity and all consulted historical inputs; failed claims also need their first unsupported block | Preserve until claim/receipt reclamation or an explicitly validated archived-prefix design replaces full historical replay; consuming a proof does not release these references |
| Retained point/span proof | Original entitlement and all historical inputs its replay/traversal consults at `Height`, including change bounds through `EndHeight` and unread ancestors | Delete the proof only after its result is consumed and bounded continuation cleanup finishes |
| Interval coordinator | Its original start/end, consumed prefix, current check height and child identity/result; genesis replay checks the entire interval up to its first failure, including inputs no longer held by a child | Consume its result into the obligation before deleting the interval and releasing its references |
| Historical scope cursor | Original insertion ordinals, creation cutoffs, heads, tombstones and change markers | No retained reader may depend on the removed ordinal/boundary; never renumber an active scope |
| Closed capacity windows | A full contiguous sequence for payable claims; the anchored remaining suffix for terminal receipts | The internal primitive removes at most 128 windows per call; the scheduler visits at most four candidates with 16 windows per visit and a fixed birth cutoff. Production activation and prefix reclamation for still-live claims remains unfinished |
| Settled claim and collateral pool | Final payments and original identity; pool reserve and payout ownership | The internal claim-to-receipt transition requires fully paid retirement, no queued/owned children and released snapshot inputs. The claim transition changes no pool amount and moves no coins. Separate internal pool reclamation requires no indexed claim or settlement, zero reserve/count/load, a deleted provider and a valid retired owner; it drains bounded denomination batches before deleting an empty pool |
| Retired pool reclamation queue | One FIFO ticket per retained retired pool, a monotonic ticket counter and exact reverse index | The internal worker gives at most four pools 16 denomination rows each per call. Pending pools rotate after the captured ticket cutoff; only final pool removal releases the entry. Preserve order through both restart modes |
| Terminal cleanup queue | Derived eligibility by original reader birth, with a fixed pass ceiling and exclusive cursor | Reconstruct candidates from settled task-free claims and all terminal receipts; preserve the pass across both restart modes. A cursor grants no history-pruning authority |
| Terminal receipt | Immutable final accounting and original birth; a bounded remaining-window cursor | No payable claim or reader may coexist. Receipts contribute zero to pool aggregates, remain readable after pool removal and cannot reopen payment. Explicit internal reclamation requires no remaining windows or reader/task references, an exact owned cleanup candidate and an identity behind the preserved allocation counter; it emits a receipt deletion without payment. | Financial event snapshot | Frozen delivery bytes and ordered replacement/deletion overlay | Complete delivery removes its private queue. Actual terminal claim/window deletions emit atomic tombstones; receipt replacements remain non-payable. Receipt reclamation emits its own deletion kind; the reference consumer covers late old fragments for all four financial namespaces, while downstream acceptance remains open |

The existing `PruneScopeBefore` primitive removes old versions while retaining a
reconstruction anchor. It neither discovers readers nor proves a safe floor.
Using the minimum height of all jobs would protect some evidence but could pin
unrelated global history; using only visited nodes would discard still-unread
dependencies. Neither is a complete retention design. Proof replay additionally
means that a retained proof's already traversed inputs cannot be discarded merely
because its live continuation no longer reads them.

Reader indexing, bounded cleanup, typed reconstruction, invariants and internal
terminal cleanup and persistent cleanup scheduling now exist. Production terminal-cleanup invocation remains disabled. Safe history
pruning must still demonstrate with independent roots that a slow claim does not

## Retained interval semantic validation

The previous proof-level checks did not validate an interval's earlier completed
spans once its child proofs had been cleaned. Five malformed inventories were
still accepted: a ready cursor skipping a known outage, invented success through
that outage, an invented earlier or later failure height, and pruning of an input
from an already consumed span. The regression fixture supplies healthy service at
height 1, outage at 2 and recovery at 3; the correct interval `[1,4)` terminates at
2. Reproduction failures are in `/tmp/structs-interval-genesis-before.log`.

`validateServiceGenesisIntervalSemantics` now recomputes the interval from its
original start using the existing historical interpreter. The check height must
be an actual boundary reached by that interpreter, and cleanup/completion metadata
must agree with the supported span or first failure at that boundary. A pending
interval is also checked through its remaining history up to completion or first
loss, without installing any of that work. Recovery cannot bridge an earlier
failure. The validation performs reads only; it does not create proof jobs,
mutate the coordinator, emit events, certify claims or move assets.

Work advances between observed change boundaries, not one block at a time.
For the same unchanged three-link graph, exporting a ready interval covering 100
blocks consumes 1,433,340 gas; covering one trillion consumes 1,433,400. Both run
under the test's 20M meter and leave every source row/event unchanged. This is a
fixed-fixture check against a height-sized loop, not a production cost bound:
more changes, larger graphs or many intervals can still grow genesis/export work.
The architecture test confines this replay to the same import/export validation
path as full proof replay.

Review also found the reverse error. A child can become `Unsupported` during
creation when its historical allocation entitlement is absent or underpowered.
If creation consumes the last work unit, the interval legitimately persists in
`VERIFY` with that completed zero-step child until its next pass. Genesis formerly
rejected this state. It now accepts this specific transition. Completed children
with traversal steps remain invalid in `VERIFY`, because the advancing call would
already have moved their interval to `CLEANUP`. Replaying the child still checks
that the zero-step refusal matches its actual historical entitlement.

Native tests cover all five new refusals atomically and the immediate-loss
transition through round-trip restoration and completion. Existing interval
phase, partial-cleanup, healthy/outage and both restart-mode tests pass. Actual
InitChain tests now replace a healthy interval with a forged completed failure,
adjust its task phase and remove its child so the shape is consistent; import
refuses after canonical initialization, preserves all five checked module stores
and events, and permits retry with the original input. `make test` passes in
`/tmp/structs-interval-genesis-full-suite.log` (app 51.409s, keeper 42.705s).
Targeted results and the quiet-span costs are in `/tmp/structs-interval-genesis-controls.log` (app 22.229s, keeper 0.685s).

No protobuf field or public transaction changed. This checks retained intervals
against the supplied journal; it does not authenticate that journal externally or
validate every accumulated financial scalar. Safe retention remains unfinished.
An interval's consumed historical prefix now explicitly remains needed for genesis
replay until its result is consumed into the obligation and the interval is
removed. Its remaining child alone is insufficient to define the retention set.
