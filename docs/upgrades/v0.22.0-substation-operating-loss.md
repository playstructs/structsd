# v0.22.0 fixed substation operating loss

September 28, 2026. This replaces the upkeep proposal as the substation change
selected for v0.22.0. There are no upkeep payments, health clocks, collateral
bounties, service-coverage purchases, new input-acceptance permissions or development
levels in this change.

## Rule

Every substation consumes 10% of its total incoming capacity:

```text
usable = floor(incoming * 9 / 10)
remaining = max(0, usable - outgoing allocation load)
connection capacity = floor(remaining / connection count)
```

The empty-station grid attribute retains the existing denominator-one convention;
no player receives power until connected, at which point shares are recalculated.
Power is rounded down in native integer units. The loss applies to the aggregate
input, not each allocation. Division before multiplication prevents uint64 overflow.

Raw `capacity` remains total incoming power in storage, public queries, exports and
`EventGrid`. It is not overwritten with usable capacity. `connectionCapacity`
reports the post-loss, post-outgoing-load share. Clients derive usable capacity
from the fixed 90% rule. No new wire fields are required.

Allocation creation, dynamic increases, automated allocation initialization,
provider headroom, connected-player shares and GridCascade all use the same usable
capacity calculation for substation sources. Player, guild and reactor primary
capacity semantics are unchanged. Existing automatic-allocation and settlement
lifecycles remain in use; the proposed upkeep-specific preservation/resizing rules
are not introduced here.

Successive stations compound the loss: 100 kW can become at most 90, then 81, then
72.9 kW. Parallel fragmentation cannot avoid the percentage loss. Tiny inputs can
round to zero usable power.

## Migration and continuation

`MigrateSubstationConnectionCapacity` runs in the existing v0.22.0 handler after
backing/power repair. For every station it:

1. Computes usable capacity from current raw incoming capacity.
2. Enqueues the station if outgoing load exceeds that usable capacity, including
   stations whose connection capacity was already zero.
3. Recalculates connection capacity and emits ordinary grid replacement events for
   changed shares.

**Migration does not destroy or resize allocations itself.** Normal EndBlock
GridCascade processes the queue with its existing work budget, allocation order,
agreement settlement and events. A large backlog continues over subsequent blocks.
Exactly 90% load is allowed and is not enqueued by this phase. Existing queue entries
are deduplicated without changing their position. All writes and events remain
inside the complete upgrade's outer rollback boundary.

An allocation can be larger than the excess that must be removed. Normal whole
allocation shedding can therefore remove more than 10% of a station's service.
Provider agreements close under their existing settlement rules when their
allocations are shed. Connected players can brown out as their shares decrease.

Genesis reconstructs raw input and shares under the same rule and restores queued
work; it also queues overloaded or closing substations if an imported queue omitted
them. Loss is never repeatedly subtracted from an exported capacity value.

## Verification scope

Regression coverage includes full-range integer arithmetic, aggregate rounding,
allocation admission and increases, automated initial power, multiple hops, player
shares, loss of external supply to a cycle, bounded legacy-chain teardown, migration
queue deduplication, migration without inline destruction, real-app upgrade/EndBlock
events, and existing upgrade rollback and both genesis restart modes.

`test_chain.sh` asserts the post-loss player share and uses the updated shared
capacity helper for guild-grid and agreement fixtures. Existing exact-power test
expectations are updated rather than disabling those checks. Full native lifecycle
and production-state upgrade rehearsal against the final release candidate remain
separate release checks; unit and real-app tests do not substitute for them.
