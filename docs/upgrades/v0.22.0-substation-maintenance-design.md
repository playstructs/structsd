# Substation development, maintenance and imbalance sabotage

Updated September 24, 2026. Current design direction, incorporating the subsequent
discussion. This supersedes the capped-upgrade Switchboards proposal. No chain
implementation is included; numerical choices below are proposed tuning values.

September 25 follow-up: the supplied *Substation Upkeep and Audit Report* evolves
this design further. See [the revision-2 review](substation-upkeep-audit-review-2026-09-25.md).
The migration section below records the user's subsequent minimum-collateral mint
decision; other sections retain the September 24 design and are not a complete
specification of the newer fuel/audit proposal.
The user also confirmed that substation output keeps declining with health during
the paid upkeep period; this is not reactor fuel and does not guarantee constant
substation output. The revision-2 review recommends the term “prepaid upkeep.”
The user also rejected scheduled health-zero shutdown: elapsed time changes
projected health and audit eligibility, while players initiate enforcement. Do not
add a due-height upkeep scan or automatic age-triggered allocation teardown. An
accepted audit or actual supply mutation uses the existing bounded GridCascade.

## Agreed direction

Substations accumulate lasting development but are never permanently finished.
They have a condition that deteriorates predictably, maintenance that restores
it, and sustainable throughput that depends on condition. Upgrades improve the
station without removing its continuing maintenance needs.

Players discover stations whose installed commitments exceed sustainable throughput
and submit an imbalance-sabotage proof. Eligibility is checked at submission time.
Owners can also sabotage their own stations. Accepted sabotage triggers bounded
repair, with one reward, rather than performing an unbounded teardown in a message.

New commitments must fit current sustainable throughput. Existing efficiency-related
overcommitment can persist until the owner corrects it or a player successfully
challenges it. Genuine upstream supply withdrawal retains ordinary GridCascade
behavior. A supplier may deliberately withdraw power and sabotage the resulting
imbalance before the cascade resolves it: the user explicitly accepts this as
gameplay. Do not add a neglect-only eligibility restriction or immunity for
supply-induced shortfalls. Submission still requires a real imbalance in the
current state; a cascade that has already corrected it defeats the challenge.

The user also accepts regional outages caused by collateral exhaustion and the
downstream loss of service. Bound processing cost, but do not limit the eventual
gameplay consequences merely because they affect several stations.

An exhausted collateral reserve is a hard exception: the station enters disrepair
and stops providing service until an authorized repair restores it. Neglect remains
something players discover; depletion is an explicit state change the chain already
knows about, so enforcing shutdown needs no upkeep scan.

There is no periodic scan of every station and no per-block financial history.
Condition is projected from the last checkpoint, elapsed blocks and a recorded
wear rate. Settle the old interval before changing its rate. Exact development,
wear, recovery, throughput and proof formulas still require the next design pass;
the old Switchboards arithmetic prototype does not validate this different model.

## Creation now funds seizable collateral

The user chose a higher founding payment with part allocated to collateral that
imbalance sabotage can seize. Each newly created station therefore starts with a
funded reward reserve, rather than relying on later maintenance payments.

Suggested initial values for balance testing:

| Founding payment | Amount | Treatment |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Total paid | 1,000,000 ualpha (1 alpha) | Required at successful creation. |
| Burned | 500,000 ualpha | Permanently removed from supply. |
| Station collateral | 500,000 ualpha | Held for accepted imbalance-sabotage rewards. |

This is ten times the 100,000-ualpha creation price in the earlier design proposal;
the currently implemented creation handler has no alpha creation charge. The user
approved the increased-cost/funded-collateral structure, not these exact prices
or percentages. Keep the price and split independently configurable in the design.

### Funding and authority

- Debit an explicitly authorized player or guild vault. A creator naming another
  owner does not gain authority to spend that owner's funds.
- Require the signing key's asset permission and the chosen vault's authority,
  alongside existing creation/source permissions.
- Burn the designated portion through the bank keeper and transfer the collateral
  portion into a module-controlled, station-specific collateral account.
- Complete creation, both transfers, permissions, allocation connection and events
  within one transaction rollback boundary. A failure leaves no partial charge or
  provisioned station.
- Keep this collateral separate from player/guild spendable balances, provider
  pools and consumer agreement collateral. A counter alone is not financial backing.

### The collateral is attached to the station

Recommended withdrawal policy: no ordinary owner withdrawal. The balance stays
with the station when it is transferred; changing owners does not reset condition,
commitments, pending reconciliation or the sabotage nonce. This prevents withdrawal just
before a challenge and avoids ownership-transfer rules that erase maintenance debt.

For voluntary deletion, the recommended default is to burn the remaining unclaimed
collateral on final deletion. Existing accepted claims remain reserved and deletion
cannot erase them. This avoids deletion/recreation becoming a collateral-refund
shortcut. This deletion rule is a design recommendation, not an already approved
user decision or implemented behavior.

### Collateral is an operating requirement, not an efficiency multiplier

Maintenance improves condition. Some maintenance spending replenishes collateral;
the rest burns. The exact maintenance split is a separate tuning parameter.

A positive pool balance does not multiply efficiency. A partial bounty therefore
does not itself reduce throughput. Exhausting the available reserve does, however,
put the station into disrepair as described below. A direct donation adds collateral,
but does not buy condition, development, special permissions or automatic recovery
from disrepair. Such deposits must never block creation at a predictable address.

Collateral-address creation must inherit the branch's protection against predictable
address account pre-creation/vesting grief. Reuse existing collateral association
events where their schema supports substations; otherwise extend the event contract
explicitly. Do not assume that a new account address is absent or unfunded.

## Sabotage and depletion

A successful challenge may seize a bounded portion of the remaining collateral,
subject to the imbalance/reward formula still to be specified. Creation funding
does not imply that every challenge takes the whole pool.

The user approved adding a small residual balance to the final sabotage reward.
Define a configurable `SabotageDustThreshold` in ualpha. With available collateral
`B > 0` and the ordinary positive reward `R` capped at `B`, compute `remaining = B - R`.
If `remaining <= SabotageDustThreshold`, award the whole `B`; otherwise award `R`.
This extra amount goes to the saboteur rather than being burned. Reserve the final
amount atomically with claim acceptance; taking the whole balance immediately
enters disrepair. Never include money reserved for another obligation in `B`.

The ordinary reward formula must produce at least one ualpha for an eligible,
funded challenge, clamped to available collateral, so integer rounding cannot
leave an unseizable residue. Keep the dust threshold below both the founding
reserve and the minimum restart reserve. Exact amounts remain balance parameters.

At acceptance, atomically move the reward into module-controlled bounty escrow and
identify the recipient and claim nonce. That money is no longer available station
collateral and cannot fund another claim or be burned during deletion. Completing
local bounded reconciliation credits the escrowed amount to the beneficiary exactly
once; downstream cascade completion is not a prerequisite. Neither withdrawing that
credit nor successful bank delivery to the beneficiary is a condition for future
sabotage or station recovery. See the failure handling below.

Bind work to the chain, target and reward recipient. Resolve and validate the
recipient's player vault at acceptance rather than accepting an arbitrary blocked
bank destination that could deliberately prevent payout. Reject healthy stations,
replayed claims and new claims while local reconciliation is pending. Shared
connection shares may be reduced during reconciliation while players remain
connected. Agreement closures must preserve checkpoint ordering, consumer priority
and exact payouts, including the explicit pending-payment path below.

Successful sabotage must leave the target neutral: remove the appropriate outgoing
allocations until retained outgoing load fits the safe service budget described
below, then recalculate `connection_capacity` from that budget's remaining capacity
and connected-player count. Complete using current supply and condition at the
completion block, not an obsolete submission snapshot. An exhausted station instead
completes offline with zero service capacity and its unsupported outgoing allocations
removed.

Before clearing the reconciliation flag, verify that the same eligibility check
would reject another challenge in that completed state. No second reward is possible
for the imbalance just repaired. Under the wear-buffer interpretation below, a
subsequent supply withdrawal or other material state change can create a new
imbalance immediately; ordinary wear must have the chosen breathing room. Automatic
resizing must respect the safe service budget and operating state, so it cannot
immediately reinstall the overload just removed. Intermediate
chunks keep the target marked as reconciling and reject new commitments that would
prevent completion; do not run an unbounded neighbor-graph proof.

### Breathing room measured in days, not a few blocks

The user approved meaningful breathing room: at least a day and potentially a week,
using an expected five-second block interval. Proposed initial
`SubstationWearBufferBlocks = 17,280`; also model `120,960` blocks for a week before
final tuning. These are block counts, so actual elapsed wall time follows actual
block production. Do not consult local wall clocks in consensus execution.

Working interpretation, pending the optional clarification: reserve capacity for
that much ordinary wear, rather than granting unconditional timed immunity. This
preserves the accepted supply-withdrawal sabotage gameplay. The exact initial value
is a proposal; the user's approved direction is the day-to-week timescale.

At a reconciliation completion height `h`, calculate the safe service budget as
the minimum projected sustainable throughput over `[h, h + bufferBlocks]`, assuming
unchanged supply and operating policy and no new maintenance. For a monotonically
declining condition curve this is the throughput at the end of that interval; use
closed-form integer arithmetic, not a loop over blocks. Shed oldest outgoing
allocations until their retained load fits that budget, then calculate shared
`connection_capacity` from what remains. Existing surviving commitments therefore
fit through the buffer interval without requiring free condition restoration.

This replaces a vaguely specified percentage tolerance with a concrete wear
allowance. Eligibility continues to require a real current imbalance; do not grant
an attacker an early challenge simply because the future forecast is lower. The
calculation for shared connection commitments must be identical in reconciliation,
readiness and sabotage eligibility, including rounding.

Apply the same conservative budget when admitting new commitments, updating shared
connection capacity or automatically resizing allocations. Otherwise an automatic
refresh could immediately spend the reserved headroom and defeat the buffer. Keep
the actual supply and sustainable-throughput attributes distinct from the usable
budget; no synthetic input power is created and no extra condition history is stored.
Simply crossing a future height does not trigger a scan or an automatic resize.

A new withdrawal or changed operating policy can invalidate the forecast and allow
an earlier attack; elapsed time alone must not do so within the chosen buffer.
If condition would reach zero within the interval, the safe budget may be zero.
Shed to that budget rather than silently healing the station; restoring useful
service then requires maintenance or another genuine improvement. Owner repair and
new-station admission should use the same forecast so they do not restart already
on the edge of another ordinary-wear challenge.

### Oldest outgoing allocations are always shed first

The user selected oldest-first shedding, without priorities or exceptions for
controller, allocation size or allocation type. Define age by original allocation
creation sequence (`Allocation.Index`), ascending. Ownership transfer, resizing and
reconnection do not make an existing allocation younger. Destroying and creating a
new allocation produces a genuinely new age and must follow ordinary settlement
and creation rules.

Use this order for sabotage shedding and the ordinary overload/disrepair cascade,
so the rules do not change depending on what triggered the loss of capacity. Process
each oldest allocation's closure before moving on to a younger one. A pending bank
payout can remain after service closure, but cannot change shedding order. If the
oldest allocation cannot be safely closed at all, retain the work for retry rather
than silently skipping it and penalizing a younger allocation.

Implementation detail: the existing source index is keyed by the textual allocation
ID, so its byte iteration order is not numeric age order (`...-10` can precede
`...-2`). Add a versioned source-scoped index keyed by fixed-width big-endian
`Allocation.Index`, with the allocation ID as value. Backfill it in the upgrade and
maintain it on creation, source changes and deletion. Iterate bounded prefixes;
do not load or sort the entire source's allocations in a transaction or block hook.

The founding deposit solves **starting with no bounty**. Repeated sabotage can
still consume the reserve, especially if the owner stops maintaining the station.
Exhaustion now ends operation instead of leaving an operating station with no bounty
incentive. Never mint a bounty or charge another pool to conceal depletion.

Self-sabotage remains legal. Balance tests must price the complete loop—founding
burn, maintenance burn, seizable collateral, hashing work and lost commitments.
The recoverable portion is not an additional alpha sink; only the burned amounts
are permanently removed from supply.

## Disrepair and return to service

Use available collateral: funds still backing the station after accepted rewards
have been moved into bounty escrow. When that amount reaches zero, mark the station
in disrepair even if the saboteur has not yet received or withdrawn the bounty.
Do not count escrow as station backing or subtract the same reward twice. Check this
on relevant collateral mutations and authorized station operations, not through a
global scan.

Disrepair has these effects:

- Sustainable service capacity and the station's shared player capacity become
  zero. Refuse new service commitments and connections while it is offline.
- Enqueue bounded GridCascade work to remove unsupported outgoing commitments.
  Shutdown is immediate at the station; downstream settlement and propagation may
  take multiple blocks. Do not promise an atomic shutdown of the entire graph.
- Reject new sabotage claims against the offline station. Complete any already
  accepted claim and its reserved payment exactly once, including failed-settlement
  retries. Exhaustion cannot cancel a bounty already earned.
- Preserve the station's identity and development. Existing player connections can
  remain attached with zero shared capacity. Agreements and allocations that are
  destroyed during shutdown must be explicitly replaced after recovery.

Recommended recovery: an authorized repair action restores condition to a defined
operating floor and funds a meaningful minimum reserve, with a burn component.
The repair price, condition floor and restart reserve remain tuning decisions;
one dust transfer should not restart the station. Ordinary donations may contribute
to that reserve but do not clear the disrepair state. Require the usual station
authority and explicit authority to spend the chosen payer's funds.

Finish local shutdown/reconciliation before allowing recommissioning, then recompute
service from current supply and condition. Do not wait for every downstream station
or for delivery of already secured payouts. Funding may arrive earlier, but it must
not cancel queued settlement, spend reserved claims or resurrect contracts. Repair
restores operating eligibility; actual energy still requires incoming supply.

### Recovery must not depend on successful payout delivery

The concrete risk in the existing code is that `AllocationCache.Destroy` first calls
agreement settlement. If the consumer transfer fails, the allocation stays alive.
Requiring every payout to succeed before finishing shutdown could therefore prevent
an owner from repairing an otherwise recoverable station indefinitely. Retrying the
same operation without changing this dependency does not solve it.

In plain terms: if a contract must close and its consumer is owed a refund, a failed
refund must neither erase the debt nor force the power connection to stay alive
forever. The fallback remembers that one amount until it is paid. It does not change
how an operating agreement earns or pays over time. The implementation concern is
that every path capable of spending the provider's money must respect that unpaid
refund. This is why the fallback needs accounting tests, not just a retry loop.

Keep this extension limited to failed terminal settlement. First reproduce payout
failures with real-app fault-injection tests, then implement only the closing state,
protected amounts and bounded retries needed for those failures. Do not add billing
history, change normal payment rates or introduce a general financial-history service.

Use the following proposed implementation, subject to accounting and real-app tests:

1. **Separate local service closure from payment delivery.** Close each selected
   allocation and agreement in an isolated atomic operation. Checkpoint the provider
   at the closure block before changing its load, calculate the final consumer and
   provider entitlements once, and remove the agreement from active billing and
   expiration processing exactly once. Ordinary active agreements retain their
   existing elapsed-block billing behavior.
2. **Preserve an unpaid obligation, not a billing history.** If the normal payout
   cannot complete, retain that same agreement as `closing`, with its fixed amount
   owed, beneficiary, denomination and closure block. It supplies no capacity and
   accrues no further service charges or cancellation penalties. Keep the minimum
   identity/index data needed to resolve it; successful final payment removes the
   closing row and its retry entry. Do not write snapshots or a row per attempt.
3. **Protect the money before releasing load.** The atomic closure must either
   pay the consumer or record the exact outstanding consumer liability and protect
   its backing in the provider pool. Provider checkpoints, revenue withdrawals,
   penalties and deletion must all honor these liabilities, with consumer claims
   ahead of provider revenue. Maintain a per-provider pending-liability aggregate
   beside the closing rows; never scan all agreements to compute spendable revenue.
   Prevent new agreements on a provider with unresolved closure payments so unpaid
   rows cannot accumulate while it repeatedly sells new service. The station itself
   may recover and support other operations or providers.
4. **Retry with bounded, persistent work.** Queue outstanding payments by identity,
   deduplicate entries, rotate failed work so one entry cannot starve others, and
   attempt each at most once per pass within a shared read/write budget. Retry from
   the fixed obligation, not by calling the original teardown again. Expose status
   and failure events; do not turn repeated failures into additional stored history.
5. **An unpaid bounty never grants sabotage immunity.** Prefund bounty escrow at
   acceptance, in the same atomic operation that starts local reconciliation. On
   completion, convert that reservation into the beneficiary's withdrawable credit,
   advance the station nonce and clear its active claim atomically. There is no bank
   transfer that can fail between crediting the beneficiary and finalizing this
   accounting step. Attempt delivery to the validated player vault separately; a
   failed delivery leaves the credit intact. A later genuinely valid sabotage can
   proceed regardless of earlier unwithdrawn credits. Aggregate unpaid credits by
   beneficiary and denomination, not one permanent row per sabotage. Keep one active
   reconciliation claim per station, prune it at completion and use events for claim
   history. Credits are fully backed by escrow, cannot be withdrawn twice, survive
   station deletion and are never spendable by its owner. Withdrawal must use the
   existing player's asset authority; retries pay only the bound beneficiary vault.
6. **Never disguise insolvency as a delivery failure.** If a provider pool actually
   lacks backing, preserve the amount owed and emit an explicit shortfall. Freeze
   provider revenue and new agreements until authorized funding repairs it; do not
   mint funds, forgive consumer debt, or use station collateral or other pools.
   Allocation service can still end with the debt recorded. Resuming station power
   does not imply its provider's financial shortfall has been fixed.

The closing record is a current unpaid obligation, not the historical billing
system previously rejected. Nevertheless this is a real extension to settlement:
genesis/export, pool solvency checks, provider deletion and permissioned payout
resolution must understand it. Do not implement a shortcut that deletes an agreement
after a failed payment or merely releases its load while losing the amount owed.
Unexpected failures before a valid closure/obligation can be committed leave the
local work pending with an explicit error; no design can safely declare arbitrary
corrupt accounting repaired by ignoring it.

Use a fresh `CurrentContext` inside each rollback boundary, commit only successful
atomic changes, and discard failed cache state. Do not resume an outer cache holding
objects that an independently committed context changed. Financial retry processing
must not re-run grid teardown, and reconnecting the station must not cancel retries.

## Existing stations and migration

Decision updated September 25: the user explicitly chose a one-time migration mint
of enough ualpha to give each existing substation minimum collateral. All stations
then follow the same operating rules; there is no separate zero-collateral legacy
exemption. Proposed target: the normal minimum restart reserve, above the final-claim
dust threshold. The exact amount remains a tuning decision.

Fund real station collateral accounts and mint only any shortfall to the chosen
target, accounting for existing usable deposits. Do not debit owners or treat the
bootstrap as a founding burn or fuel purchase. Define the treatment of stations
already pending deletion, publish mint/funding and address-association events, and
reconcile the aggregate bank-supply increase. Rehearse atomic upgrade rollback and
restart so the one-time operation cannot double-fund stations.

This resolves the zero-collateral shutdown conflict. It does not remove the newer
proposal's initial reduction to 70% unfuelled throughput; that separate gameplay
effect still needs migration rehearsal and clear communication.

No station-ID ordering restriction is proposed. Retain open multi-station routing.
However, the earlier proof about lossy *settled* loops cannot establish safety of
this model's deliberately persistent overcommitments. Cycle behavior must be
reviewed under these actual rules, including connected-player shares and deferred
repairs, rather than borrowing the automatic-reconciliation proposal's acceptance.

## Implementation steps once the rules are settled

1. Specify development, condition, operating-size selection, throughput, eligibility,
   difficulty and reward formulas. Size-driven maintenance must depend on an
   owner-authorized operating choice, not unsolicited input that outsiders can alter.
2. Model the full maintenance/self-sabotage economy, final reserve depletion,
   restart costs, supplier-induced sabotage and cyclic networks. Establish a real
   migration funding policy and parameter values. Compare 17,280- and 120,960-block
   wear buffers, including the resulting usable energy and maintenance cadence.
3. Add current station state and the collateral association through `proto/` and
   regeneration; implement cached projections and read-only previews without logs.
   Add and backfill the numeric oldest-first source index.
4. Implement atomic funded creation using existing vault authority and bank patterns.
   Add minimum/maximum payment constraints where a transaction needs quote protection.
5. Add maintenance, development and explicit disrepair recovery with
   settle-before-mutate ordering. Gate service on operating state and implement
   bounded shutdown without deleting the station or its lasting development.
6. Add submission-time sabotage validation, escrowed rewards and bounded repair,
   including the final dust seizure, oldest-first shedding, the wear buffer and
   verified neutral completion. Implement and
   test atomic closing obligations and protected provider liabilities before allowing
   service recovery independent of payment delivery; preserve all existing consumer
   priority, provider checkpoint and exactly-once protections.
7. Publish truthful creation-payment, burn, collateral-address, balance, claim,
   completion, payout, payment-pending, financial-shortfall, disrepair and
   return-to-service events. Distinguish available from reserved collateral and
   service closure from payment delivery. Downstream indexer implementation remains
   separate.
8. Rehearse migration/export/restart and update `test_chain.sh`, economic invariants,
   real-app permissions, failure rollback, dust/pre-created account, repeated claim,
   empty-pool, final-reward shutdown, dust donation, repair during pending settlement,
   restart and large-graph tests. Add boundary cases below/at/above the dust threshold,
   supply-withdrawal sabotage, same-state second-claim rejection, auto-resize after
   repair, failed consumer/bounty delivery, successful later sabotage despite an
   unwithdrawn bounty, escrow backing and double-withdrawal rejection, numeric
   oldest-first order across ID digit boundaries and processing chunks, retry
   starvation, pending-liability pool
   protection, frozen final amounts across retries/restart, and recovery with a
   downstream settlement still outstanding. Check constant-state wear for the full
   day/week interval, earliest eligibility at the boundary, immediate supply attacks,
   zero future throughput, and headroom preservation after automatic updates. The
   current open cache and gas findings remain
   prerequisites to shipping another feature in these paths.

Creation collateral and depletion-driven disrepair are the latest design additions.
This document records the design direction and implementation requirements; it does not claim that the full
maintenance/sabotage mechanism or its release economics have been finalized.
