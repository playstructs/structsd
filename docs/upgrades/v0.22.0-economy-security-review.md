## Policy resolutions, 2026-09-21

Rank-derived authority ends when current membership/rank no longer supplies it;
owner-derived authority follows the current guild owner. Independent direct
grants remain until explicitly revoked, including self-directed grants made
using rank authority. Departure, dismissal, demotion and ownership transfer do
not require new retrospective cleanup. Grant provenance and cascading revocation
are out of scope. Earlier unresolved child-grant policy notes below are closed;
ordinary authorization, signing-key ceilings and explicit revocation still need
regression coverage. This decision does not authorize unrelated permission
expansion or redesign existing cross-guild delegation rules.

Native SDK vesting accounts with retained validator self-stake or associated
pending records will remain intact at their existing addresses, including their
other assets and stake. Vesting obligations stay with the SDK account; do not
add custom ownership-transfer, split-schedule or coordinated-counter machinery.
Other player-associated addresses still migrate. The shared address plan now
implements this exception; 16 real-SDK cases cover all four native account types,
pending-only positions, native access/refunds and slashing. Final combined
upgrade, later address lifecycle and continuation verification remain required.
Earlier schedule-splitting requirements below are superseded.

## Allocation deletion must preserve both authority paths through ante

ante map on both 117b and 118b nevertheless required `SourceAllocation` for every
delete. That disabled the documented object-level fallback for a delete-only
key, including a guild owner's scoped key or a member's explicit rank grant.

availability/least-privilege defect, not evidence of unauthorized deletion.

modify the working tree while comparing behavior.

fixed `SourceAllocation`, fixed `Delete` nor an AND of both expresses its policy.

and resizing retain their fixed `SourceAllocation` ante requirements. Registration,

## Allocation power records must leave with their allocation

The teardown regression reproduced a raw `5-6-1` power row surviving allocation
deletion. Repeating creation/deletion could accumulate one such row for each
monotonic allocation ID. This was orphaned storage; the existing Deleted and
existence guards already prevented the removed allocation from supplying power.
Source load and destination capacity had already been reduced, so repairing the
orphan must not subtract that power a second time.

`AllocationCache.Destroy` now clears its power attribute through the same
CurrentContext grid cache used by updates. Removing it directly from the store
would be insufficient: allocation commits precede grid commits, and a pending
power-cache write could recreate the row. The tests exercise both a previously
loaded value and a changed-but-uncommitted value, repeated teardown, surviving
load, refusal through stale handles and exactly one zero-value `EventGrid`.
The 28 signed deletion scenarios additionally verify raw-key absence on success,
correct grid/deletion events, and restoration of the power row with no clear
event when a later message fails.

`MigrateOrphanedAllocationPower` is part of the assembled v0.22.0 handler before
vault relocation, module migrations and service initialization. Its keeper
operation walks only the allocation-power namespace, preserves every key whose
allocation row exists, and clears only absent allocations' power records. It
does not alter source/destination aggregates, allocation counters or live
allocation rows, and does not enqueue a cascade. Iterators close before writes.
Each pass retains at most 128 candidate keys in memory; the **total migration
still scales with that namespace's inventory**. An architecture guard confines
the full walk to this upgrade phase, not transactions or block hooks.

A 270-row fixture crosses multiple batch boundaries, removes 180 orphan rows

## Reclaimed receipts and identity reuse

Receipt removal cannot rely on the receipt itself to prevent replay. The new
internal cleanup checks that the canonical agreement/allocation identity is
behind the retained allocation counter, and allocation creation now refuses
counter exhaustion rather than wrapping. Restart controls preserve that counter
while the old receipt stays absent.

Ordinary cache creation also needed a replacement guard: a missing receipt no
longer blocks a forged new claim by its mere presence. Payable claims now require
explicit reservation/migration or genesis admission, new receipts require
archive/genesis admission, and windows require their retained claim unless being
restored by genesis. A loaded claim cannot change its agreement identity to reuse
another claim's admission. These are keeper API safeguards; this review does not
claim an externally signed exploit of the former cache behavior.

Removal repeats detached-reader and window-completion checks, moves no funds,
and emits a receipt-specific deletion. Gas failure rolls removal and its event
back together. Late frozen financial snapshots cannot resurrect the deleted
receipt. Receipt dispatch is integrated with the bounded fixed-birth queue; production activation remains outstanding; see
the [reclamation contract](v0.22.0-history-retention-design.md#completed-receipt-reclamation-implemented).

from masquerading as the original complete traversal.

No live collector creates these proofs or assumes ownership of their observations.
They are supply evidence for one span, not payment receipts or a full claim's
retention certificate. The [durable proof contract](v0.22.0-history-retention-design.md#durable-dependency-proofs-implemented)
records the remaining admission, parent/child protection and pruning requirements.

## Settlement interval ownership before retention admission

Generic interval mutation methods now refuse settlement-owned IDs; only the
coordinator can create, advance and retire those intervals. Completed evidence
must remain available until its owner certifies it. Genesis and the standing
reachability invariant reject reserved intervals with no owning task. This mirrors
the existing isolation of interval child proofs and prevents future admission
code from trusting an ownership boundary that unrelated keeper callers can bypass.
There is no public transaction/query mutation endpoint for these APIs; this is
internal lifecycle hardening, not evidence of a remote transaction exploit.

Real-app tests cover absent, pending, completed and deleted interval states,
atomic refusal, exact automatic settlement and an orphan invisible to collateral
solvency. The existing bad-frontier regression still reaches the intended
certification refusal after restoring a consistent owner/interval graph. See the
[admission ownership boundary](v0.22.0-history-retention-design.md#admission-ownership-boundary)
for evidence, compatibility and remaining protection requirements.

## Reader snapshot identity and mutation ordering

The private reader snapshot now preserves the first canonical basis before any
update or deletion, atomically with that lifecycle's root and enrollment writes.
A reader recreated under the same object ID has a new birth sequence and cannot
replace the original. A separate retained snapshot generation prevents a stale
cursor or cleanup request from acting on a later pass, even at the same seal and
reader ceiling. Discard retains this counter; overflow refuses rather than
reusing an identity. These address lifecycle and stale-state classes highlighted
by the earlier cache/index reviews.

Snapshot basis validation checks enum values before narrowing. Genesis validates
original claim-window capacity against retained canonical windows, distinguishes
legitimate same-block unsealed repricing, and refuses inconsistent generation or
reader counts atomically. Real InitChain tests cover both height modes and
unchanged bank/auth/staking/distribution state on refusal. Gas failures cannot
leave half a preimage write or half a discard. Bounded pagination remains
constant under later births. Detailed evidence and limits are in the
[snapshot contract](v0.22.0-history-retention-design.md#immutable-reader-snapshots-implemented).

This metadata does not authenticate an externally rewritten enrollment history,
prove a full transitive dependency set or authorize payout/pruning. The live
snapshot-start call remains prohibited by the architecture test until admission
and dependency ownership are implemented. Existing financial replay and pruning
guards remain necessary.

### S9. Rate history must conserve stake-backed energy

Fuel conversion now truncates the decimal division as well as the final integer.
An exact-rational regression reproduced a value just below one being rounded to
one by `LegacyDec.Quo` before `TruncateInt`. `QuoTruncate` removes that intermediate
round-up; boundary cases and comparison with SDK `TokensFromSharesTruncated`
cover the repair. Native staking redemption and vesting counters retain SDK
semantics rather than inheriting this gameplay accounting change.

The interrupted-restoration regression combines real partial recovery, a 5%
staking slash, a second jail and subsequent rebond across 130 positions. Pending
restoration and slash reconciliation share the queue but resolve current
eligibility on each position: all output stays zero while jailed. Subsequent
recovery preserves the reduced backing and both 100% and 55% historical weights
without duplication. Owner capacities and SDK accounting are checked as well.

History: `1c2eb18`, `4710499`, `4cacae8`, `d3e3025`, `858cfec`.

- Use deterministic arithmetic with checked intermediates and specified
  rounding. Integer storage alone does not prevent multiplication overflow or
  exploitable rounding. Do not use grid saturation to hide invalid financial math.
- A top-up prices only added stake. Partial withdrawal, merge, migration,
  redelegation, slash, cancellation and jail/restart all need a defined rate
  transition. A saved effective ratio overwritten by jail-to-zero loses history.
- Test conservation across split/merge cycles and different operation orders;
  do not round every small position up. Slash reconciliation must not interpret
  changed share value as newly deposited stake at today's band.
- Define band ties, small validator sets, active-set membership and snapshot
  timing deterministically. Preserved rates can weaken the ongoing incentive
  against concentration; this is an economic design question, not proof of a bug.

capacity and allocation plus struct load. The previous subtraction guard still

rejecting available power. Saturating both totals would also hide overload when
both exceed `MaxUint64`; only the final nonnegative available amount is clamped
to the public uint64 return type. `GetAvailableCapacity`, `IsOnline` and
`CanSupportLoadAddition` share this calculation. The regression reproduces the
old failures and checks seven boundary states against an independent `big.Int`
oracle, including requests of zero and `MaxUint64`. This establishes correctness
for representable grid attributes, not transaction reachability of those extreme

### S10. Guild scale cannot choose block execution cost

The adjacent infusion destruction phase is independently bounded to 128 raw

## Retained ordinary self-stake in mixed vesting wallets

`BuildVaultVestingMigrationPlan` previously counted all ordinary source stake as
vault `LiquidDelegated`. Once operator self-stake was retained, that included
stake the vault never received. In a wallet with another address's vesting
schedule, later refunds consumed those phantom free counters before reducing
`DelegatedVesting`; the residual vesting offset could unlock coins early.

`TestVestingVaultCountersExcludeRetainedOrdinarySelfStake` reproduced 12M free
counters instead of the 2M actually moving. The planner now applies the same
self-stake destination rule to both bonded shares and pending unbondings before
constructing counters. The regression migrates a mixed wallet, undelegates all
actual vault stake through the native SDK, completes both unbondings, and checks
free/vesting counters are exhausted and the original 1M vesting amount is locked.
Retained vesting self-stake remains a separate unimplemented schedule-splitting
case; this fix must not be presented as closing that gate.

## Operator self-stake across address changes

The ordinary assembled delegation transfer cannot rekey native self-stake:
minimum-self-delegation and unjail checks resolve the operator's account address.
`delegationTransferBlocker` now identifies this case before distribution or queue
checks. Primary rotation uses `DelegationTransferStrictRetainSelf`, retaining
self-stake at the still-registered old primary while remaining strict for all
other blockers. Unknown policies do not acquire disown behavior.

Revocation keeps the existing disown policy: native stake stays at the operator,
its infusion stops crediting the player, and the address association is removed.
This is not cryptographic rotation of a validator operator key; native staking
continues to require that key. The real SDK rotation/revocation regression checks
this distinction, reward access before revocation and preserved delegation shares.
Incoming-operator registration now uses the retain-self policy and reconciles
the retained source after all other transfer hooks have committed. That reconcile
resolves ownership through the caller's CurrentContext, which already contains
the pending registration index, avoiding a stale owner read from the store. A real
SDK regression revokes and re-registers a bonded operator with a fresh key proof
to another player; exact delegation shares, capacity transferred after commission,
old-owner isolation, reward access and proof nonce consumption are checked.
Vault/fee routing still needs completion.

## Vault-to-key fee funding

`PlayerSend` accepts a provisioned vault source using `GetVaultOwner` and the

retain their previous player-wallet authorization. The subject source never

vault spend rolls back the whole Structs batch and its transfer events. This
establishes a fee-bootstrap operation, not an automatic fee payer or permission
for a vault to sign native transactions.

## Weighted delegation relocation

Reconciliation at a new delegator address previously treated moved fuel as a
new deposit. A primary-rotation regression demonstrated a merged weight of 10B
instead of 15B for 1M fuel at 100% plus 1M at 50%. The transfer now preflights
histories through direct store reads before removal hooks commit, then installs
the combined position through the caller's cache after all transfer hooks finish.
It does not populate an outer grid cache before those nested commits. Subsequent
normal reconciliation updates pending balances without repricing the move.

The arithmetic bounds combined fuel to the sum of both floor-rounded positions
or one more and uses wide integers. That recovered unit receives the combined
blend, rounded down. Fractional-only positions have no stored rate to combine. Their at-most-one
newly recognized fuel unit now receives the minimum 50% rate; a zero weight
cannot justify a grandfathered bonus. A real SDK slash/primary-rotation test
reproduced the former strict refusal and verifies intact shares, one fuel unit,
weight 5000 and zero rounded power. A history-preparation
failure under revocation instead follows the existing disown policy: retain stake
at the source, remove its capacity/association and allow key eviction. Tests cover
the grandfathered merge, rounding/overflow bounds and nonblocking revocation when
no published rate can reconcile a stale weighted position. Signed weighted primary rotation now covers BaseApp/ante success and failure at
message index 1. Exact delegation/infusion rows, identity, balances, capacity and
transaction-event rollback are checked. Broader fractional/slashing combinations
and registration/revocation signed-batch rollback remain outstanding.

when the read/refusal context commits. The source still needs a bounded proof of
slash-adjusted commission amounts; validator eligibility alone does not establish
that all commission is backed or replace the shutdown scan.

### Rejected commission proof: delegation count cannot switch off a reactor

A candidate bounded destination-infusion scan correctly projected the 5% slash
in the real-app fixture, preserving per-position entry rates and commission
rounding. It was removed from live admission and ancestry before acceptance:
requiring a complete inventory within 2,048 units makes an otherwise healthy
reactor's commission capacity unusable once its infusion count exceeds the
allowance. Unlike a player's own address inventory, reactor delegation count is
not controlled by the reactor operator. Delegators can fragment their positions,
so an admission limit here introduces a third-party denial-of-service condition.
The passing small-state slash experiment is not proof that this design is safe.

The current production behavior therefore retains the constant-work validator
health gate, and slash-adjusted commission backing remains unresolved. The next
design must distinguish real eligibility/accounting transitions from voluntary
restart requests, track the revision being repaired, and prevent completion of an
older pass from certifying newer state. Healthy capacity must not become unusable
merely because another delegation was added or a repair was requested. Repair
progress and export/import must preserve that distinction. Any aggregate shortcut
must also prove per-position rounding and exact-once settlement; multiplying a
rounded commission total by the slash fraction is not generally exact.

The commission test fixture now initializes all retained reactor positions before
activation, including the validator's pre-existing self-stake. Initializing only
the newly created player position had left a legacy unweighted row in an otherwise
active fixture, which does not represent the required upgrade ordering.

### Repair completion must not depend on future delegation arrivals

`TestReactorSlashQueue_LivenessWithArrivals` measures the current worker's raw
queue traversal. An initial 256-entry job completes in two processing calls
without arrivals. Adding 256 later-sorting entries before each of calls 2–5
raises the inventory to 1,280 and moves completion to call 10. Work per call
stays bounded and the queue drains once arrivals stop. The mock intentionally
does not perform real staking reconciliation; this is evidence about job scope,
not about repaired balances. That initial experiment predated finite snapshots. The test now requires
completion at call 2 with or without later arrivals.

Consequently a new commission gate cannot simply remain closed until the current
queue is empty. Arrivals could prolong a power outage independently of the slash
that began the repair. Capturing the highest address is also insufficient as a
general bound: new keys can be inserted between the cursor and that address.

The next implementation needs a finite pre-transition inventory. A candidate is
a per-reactor monotonically assigned insertion ordinal, with a scoped ordinal-to-
infusion index and reverse key maintained on creation/deletion. Capturing the
current ordinal at a real slash gives the worker a fixed upper bound; later
creations have higher ordinals and their ordinary hooks reconcile live staking.
This index must rebuild deterministically during migration/genesis, preserve
existing ordinals on ordinary updates, remove exactly the written key on deletion,
and never reuse an ordinal on recreation. It must not add a chain-wide scan to
live admission or hooks. The insertion index and bounded reader are now
implemented along with the repair snapshot/worker integration described below.

Acceptance also requires distinguishing actual accounting transitions from
manual restart requests; a newer transition superseding an older repair; explicit
success/failure propagation before any repair is certified; arrivals, deletion
and recreation during repair; fair multi-reactor progress; and export/import plus
atomic migration rollback. Arithmetic failures now reach the queue worker and
retain their retry position, as described below. Successful cursor exhaustion
still needs a complete reconciliation-outcome audit before it can certify all
accounting, including missing reactor mappings and callers outside this worker.

### Failed reactor reconciliation remains queued

The real-app failure regression injects an invalid stored efficiency weight.
Previously reconciliation logged the error and the worker removed the reactor
from its queue. Weighted reconciliation and initialization errors now return to
the worker. It processes the rest of the bounded batch, then retains the first
failed address instead of advancing past it or declaring completion. Unparseable
infusion addresses also retain a retry. Corrupt rows may require state repair;
retrying does not make them valid or imply a later block will fix them.

`TestReactorRepairRetainsFailedPositionAndRetriesWithoutDoubleCredit` verifies the
failed row remains unchanged, another row in the same real-staking batch restores
its power, and correction followed by retry drains the queue without another
grid delta or public event. Native staking/distribution accounting checks pass.
The queue unit test fills a batch with malformed addresses: its retry stays at
the first failed row, while round-robin scheduling completes another reactor on
the following call. A manual restart cannot bypass the retained retry cursor.

This preserves the existing per-call work bound and cross-reactor scheduling.
Repeated failure can delay later batches of the same reactor, and the finite
pre-transition inventory plus commission-certification state remain unfinished.
Non-queue staking callers retain their existing logging behavior; this change
does not claim a complete error-propagation audit of every staking lifecycle.

### Finite repair snapshots are wired into the worker

Each newly queued reconciliation now captures a per-reactor insertion cutoff and
starts an exclusive ordinal cursor at zero. Batches read only that interval and
persist their last completed ordinal. A failed position retains the ordinal
immediately before it, so it is retried without expanding the original cutoff.
The existing address field remains diagnostic: it records the last processed
address or first failed address, not the authoritative resume position.

Manual restart requests preserve a pending interval. A new reconciliation request
resets the cursor and captures the current insertion end. Completion and removal
of a missing reactor delete both queue and snapshot rows. Invalid snapshot/index
encodings retain the job and log failure rather than certifying it. Index-read
failure consumes the remaining work allowance conservatively. The round-robin
reactor position still advances so another reactor can run next.

The arrivals regression now completes its 256-position job in two calls even
after 256 additional positions are inserted. Deletion/recreation of an unvisited
address removes its old ordinal and leaves the recreation outside the earlier
job. Tests verify manual restart preserves that interval and a new pass captures
the later ordinal. Rebuilding insertion order resets pending snapshots to the new
canonical interval and clears their obsolete diagnostic cursor; the real-app
migration test exercises this with deliberately stale numeric state.

Focused signed restart, repeated-small-reactor fairness, correction/retry, and
pending slash/restoration export-import tests pass with this worker. Snapshot
state is not exported because genesis rebuilds reactor accounting. Explicit
snapshot-key rollback/restart assertions, native arrivals during a real slash,
and the distinction between repair progress and commission eligibility still
need coverage. A completed snapshot is not yet an implemented commission
certificate, and these changes do not replace the unbounded shutdown scan.

## Archive reuse ownership review

partially disposed snapshots. Duplicate proof births, changed roots or ranges,
missing owners, inconsistent holds and missing index rows are refused.

by recovery, growing windows, paid-prefix refusal/release, partial disposal,

and scope prune floors still deny unavailable history. Span reads stop at an
absence interval's end, preventing a proof from silently crossing a gap with no
next-change row. Initial-absence retention checks both indexed evidence and the
ordinary reader, so a broken head or scope inventory cannot be promoted into a
valid absence record. It cannot promote post-presence tombstones.

Genesis refuses duplicate/unordered identities, unknown capture namespaces,
invalid intervals, overlaps with retained births/versions and intervals crossing
prune floors. Empty defaults preserve previous semantics. The boundary and
retention methods have no block-hook callers. The following archive-owned pass
now supplies their internal caller, enforced by the architecture guard, and checks
ownership, admission and completion. Bounded release of unused absence records
and deletion of point facts/empty scope headers remain required before activation.


## Archive-owned absence preservation review

The new internal pass freezes the entire retained archive inventory after
snapshot handoff. Existing reverse dependency rows authenticate each owner; reused
and inherited coverage participates through its original immutable archive. A
partial archive cursor cannot be mistaken for a completed one. Both archive and
dependency counts must agree with indexed EOF, and genesis checks every processed
dependency's required explicit absence rather than trusting the progress counter.
Only complete coverage can publish the new availability boundary atomically.

Archive creation/retirement/dependency deletion is excluded while the pass owns
its inventory. Incomplete preservation blocks pruning and snapshot disposal;
completed preservation releases with disposal. Mutation of existing borrowing or
snapshot holds does not change the immutable dependency set. Work is bounded by
four slices, sixteen dependencies per slice, the shared allowance and the existing
copy byte budget. Ordinary errors and gas exhaustion roll back both evidence and
progress; corrupt ownership can never advance a cutoff by being skipped. Retired
cleanup receipts have no remaining proof and are charged as visited headers.

The archive count is derived metadata, updated with header creation/removal and
reconstructed from actual genesis rows. The complete export and invariant checks
reject mismatches. Initial absence remains historical input, not a new supply
verdict or checkpoint. Old supported and unsupported results remain unchanged in
the focused fixture; canonical assets and events also remain unchanged. Bounded
release of unowned absence records, actual point/header deletion, financial
integration, total resource measurements and live activation remain open.


## Obsolete absence evidence release review

Cleanup uses the same exact dependency ownership floor as version retention,
under complete snapshot handoff and admission coverage. It removes an interval
only when every retained requirement begins at or beyond its end and the
published availability cutoff covers the whole interval. Shared archives,
borrowed coverage and snapshot holds continue to protect original proof heights.
An orphan index or partial archive retirement is a refusal, never proof of
non-ownership. Reads below the availability floor then refuse unavailable
history instead of inventing absence; present-time missing objects remain readable.

The completed preservation owner excludes new absence identities and archive
retirement while the finite cleanup inventory is traversed. Count and cursor
changes share the deletion cache; gas exhaustion cannot publish half a removal.
Ordinary candidate errors retain evidence and rotate, while structural failures
refuse the whole call. Genesis independently counts the retained prefix and
requires the completed preservation owner. The deleted-cursor flag is scheduling
metadata, not authority for another deletion. Empty inventories leave neither
absence rows nor a derived count row. No canonical resource or event changes.

Production activation still requires point/header reclamation, financial cleanup
integration, total block-cost measurements and the broader release matrix.


## Point tombstone reclamation review

A tombstone after earlier presence is still explicit historical evidence. The
point worker excludes every exact reader before the published absence cutoff
before it may delete that tombstone; it cannot substitute an initial-absence
interval. Complete preservation and admission/handoff coverage establish the
cutoff. No owner search scans all readers. Live facts and later tombstones are
skipped before redundant pruning, and actual deletion still requires a single
retained tombstone and change marker with authenticated birth/ordinal membership.

The point collection's sparse-scan floor does not govern exact point reads.
Otherwise deleting one unowned point would deny an older, protected read of a
different point in the same domain. Old scans now refuse, while retained exact
values/tombstones keep their own original evidence and per-fact floors. Both
count trees and all point metadata are updated in the same cache; a gas panic
cannot leave a hole with stale counts. Rebirth uses a fresh ordinal and birth,
so an interrupted finite pass cannot treat new state as its former cursor.

Genesis requires an availability boundary covering every point scan floor and
a completed preservation owner for an in-progress deleted point frontier.
Creation cleanup removes only counted empty ranges and keeps boundaries needed
by old retained points. Actual InitChain and database continuation controls cover
both clock modes. This does not delete object-specific empty scope headers or
authorize financial cleanup or production scheduling.

## Scope replay retention correction

A retained absence is not sufficient evidence that its inventory position can
be destroyed. The proof interpreter spends a step on each scoped tombstone;
genesis validates exact execution progress and archive step counts. A new
real-app regression reproduced export failure after reclaiming a tombstone older
than its owning proof. This is a chain-continuation defect even though balances
and the final supported result are unchanged.

Scoped deletion now requires no snapshot or durable whole-scope owner. It also
requires admission closed past the snapshot seal and no later seal, excluding
new independent scans that the frozen snapshot cannot cover. Version pruning
continues; the finite deletion pass retains these candidates and rotates. Both
ownership checks are indexed, and pruning/deletion retain their enclosing SDK
cache for rollback. Existing gas-failure controls exercise the new guard too.

That first seal barrier was conservative and prevented reclamation across
advancing blocks. The observation design below replaces it. Safe empty-header
deletion remains required. Neither relaxed genesis replay nor cursor
normalization is an acceptable substitute. The worker still has no production
caller; this correction changes no assets or events.

## Scope observation ownership

A scope records the greatest enrollment sequence of a durable proof that
successfully scanned its existing insertion positions. An observer newer than a
snapshot's fixed reader ceiling prevents that snapshot from deleting the scope's
tombstones. Covered observers remain subject to the existing snapshot/archive
ownership checks. This is one monotonic scalar per scope, without an unbounded
per-proof lease cleanup or a scan of later readers. Later proof creation alone
does not freeze unobserved scopes; the observation and saved proof progress
commit together when the scope is first read.

The recorder prepares its write before interpretation and publishes only after
the step validates. Ordinary failure does not create an observation, gas failure
rolls back the entire batch, and read-only views do not publish. A scope absent
at the historical observation height has no old insertion positions to protect,
even if its header is created before later genesis replay.

Genesis checks both chronology bounds and actual replayed observations. Live
proofs use their current birth; archived proofs use the saved original proof
sequence, not their temporary replay enrollment. A forged lower watermark must
not turn a later observer into apparent non-ownership. Such snapshots refuse
atomically. The watermark persists after proof retirement until a later complete
snapshot can resolve that birth, avoiding an unbounded cleanup inside proof
disposal. It never replaces archive ownership or the historical availability
floor. No asset movement or event contract changes.


## Empty-scope header ownership and continuation

Header deletion now requires the same indexed absence of scan-position owners as
scoped fact reclamation, plus the completed published absence boundary. Empty
inventory does not mean unowned inventory: a later proof can have saved its high
water mark even after all facts are gone. The new regression retains that header
through the old pass, verifies exact proof replay, and permits removal only after
the observer retires and a later snapshot covers its birth.

A separate pass takes ownership after creation cleanup releases its immutable
birth inventory. It decrements birth counts with deletion and records whether
its saved cursor was removed. Genesis validates remaining counts and the visited
prefix; ordinary readers cannot opt into a missing cursor. Reusing a scope name
creates a later birth outside the frozen pass. Missing parents, overlapping
creation cleanup and inconsistent cursor/count state refuse import atomically.
Parents cannot be released while this child still owns them. A gas panic during
header removal rolls back metadata, count trees and the frontier together.

Normal and zero-height actual InitChain plus repeated database reopenings cover
deleted-cursor continuation. Historical absence below the published boundary
still refuses. No bank movements, events or permission changes are introduced,
and the internal cycle still has no production caller. This closes the header
retention implementation step without establishing total resource bounds or
approving financial cleanup activation.


## Financial cleanup scheduling and ownership

The maintenance coordinator rotates through history, paid-prefix, terminal, pool
and archive work. Each turn passes the remaining shared allowance to a worker,
skipping empty lanes at a cost of one unit each and at most five probes total.
It stops after one busy, held or failed worker. Rotation
persists even after an ordinary lane error, but a gas panic rolls back rotation
and all work together. Unknown lane values refuse. A held snapshot or failed
candidate cannot indefinitely consume every cleanup turn.
An all-idle circuit writes no new scheduling state. Controls cover partial idle
rotation through genesis, exhausting the allowance before financial work, and
gas failure after three idle probes followed by a paid-window deletion. The
outer SDK cache rolls back those probes, the deletion and the saved lane together.

The paid-prefix queue is derived from claim state and keyed by authenticated
reader birth. Eligibility only means verified closed windows remain; it conveys
no authority to erase unpaid windows. Each candidate still runs the archival
operation's payment, contiguity, snapshot, child and settlement checks. Queue
entries leave when no verified suffix remains or the claim retires; archival
removes them before unenrolling the original birth. Genesis reconstructs the
index from financial records and verifies the finite pass bounds. Export and
the reader invariant detect missing and orphan entries independently of a saved
cursor. Later births cannot extend the current pass.

Composed controls preserve live financial totals while historical evidence,
paid prefixes, terminal records and retired pools move through their existing
ownership gates. Normal/zero-height InitChain and database reopen preserve the
partial paid pass and next lane. No permission checks or payment authorization
are relaxed. The coordinator remains internal; production activation must replace
the standalone archive-release block call and satisfy aggregate resource and
downstream acceptance requirements.
